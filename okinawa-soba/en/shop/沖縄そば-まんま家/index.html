Okinawa Soba Manmaya

沖縄そば まんま家
Furujima, NahaRating 10/15

A popular shop near Naha City Hospital in Furujima, Naha. You can choose the soup from three types: rich pork bone / light / light bonito, and the noodles from wavy / straight. The signature "Manmaya Soba" is hearty with pig's feet, braised pork belly, and soft-bone pork ribs, and comes with jushi and two side dishes. A shop bustling with local customers.

On this Okinawa soba journey, I visited "Okinawa Soba Manmaya" in Furujima, Naha City 🍜 I ate here in June 2025 😄

It's a shop in the Furujima neighborhood with a red sign that catches the eye. The storefront is what you'd call a neighborhood Okinawa soba shop, and its unpretentious atmosphere actually raises my expectations 😄 Partly because I visited at lunchtime, the shop was quite bustling with local customers. Being loved by this many locals seems like proof of deliciousness in itself 😊

Now, for my order I naturally chose the signature menu item named after the shop, "Manmaya Soba." The interesting thing about this shop is that you can choose the soup and the noodles separately. You can choose the soup from three types, "rich pork-bone broth," "light broth," and "light bonito broth," and the noodles from "wavy noodles" and "straight noodles."

I hesitated, but this time I had them combine "light bonito broth" for the soup with the "wavy noodles" that the staff told me "are popular." Being able to ask for recommendations like this is a pleasure unique to a local shop 😋

Incidentally, the menu also had taco rice, a lineup unexpected (lol) for an Okinawa soba shop.

And here's the bowl that arrived. A quite hearty bowl with pig's feet (tebichi), braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), and soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki) piled on ✨

First, the soup. It's named "light," but when I drank it the bonito broth came through firmly, and it was a bowl with umami rather than just lightness. It's fairly tasty 😋 The rich pork bone is tempting too, but the balance of this bonito broth was pretty good as well.

I chose the wavy noodles. Surprisingly, and I say this with apologies, for a shop with such an unpretentious storefront they're authentic, and the chewy texture is fun 🍜 They clung well to the soup and were fairly tasty.

The toppings are pig's feet, braised pork belly, and soft-bone pork ribs, with volume to spare. However, the soft-bone pork ribs were finished with a firm, crunchy texture, which was a little disappointing for me, someone who likes meltingly tender ^^; Both the soft-bone pork ribs and braised pork belly had a fair amount of sinewy texture. This might be where opinions divide.

And what's nice is that when you order soba, jushi and two small side dishes (mozuku and macaroni salad) come with it.

This time I ordered the "large," so the soba alone is plenty hearty, and with the jushi and side dishes added I'm completely full 😆 With this much volume, it's understandable that local customers never stop coming. The only disappointment was perhaps that there's no parking lot. I parked on the road along a nearby park, but if you're going by car you'll probably need to look for a nearby coin parking lot 🚗

Rating (10/15): The light bonito broth is a soup with firm broth and umami, and the wavy noodles are more authentic and chewy than they look. With pig's feet, braised pork belly, and soft-bone pork ribs it's full of volume, and the satisfaction of having jushi and side dishes is plentiful. The crunchy texture of the soft-bone pork ribs and the lack of parking may divide opinions, but it was a bowl for which it's understandable why it's bustling with locals 😊
