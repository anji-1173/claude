Jitchaku, Urasoe (inside the National Theatre Okinawa)Rating 9/15

A one-of-a-kind shop inside the National Theatre Okinawa. While flying a banner for "the original Ryukyu suba", it offers soy sauce ramen made with medium-thick fresh Okinawa soba noodles, Taiwanese ramen, and even lu rou fan — a shop of "ramen made with Okinawa soba noodles".

I went to eat here in January 2025 😁😊

The National Theatre Okinawa in Jitchaku, Urasoe City. Inside this facility, where you can fully enjoy Okinawa's traditional culture, there's a slightly unusual Okinawa soba shop. That's "Waza-ya".

🎭This time I got the "soy sauce ramen with lu rou fan" set. The banner proudly says "Ryukyu suba", so this definitely counts toward my Okinawa soba tour 😄

When you enter the National Theatre Okinawa building, you see, a more modern and refined space than you'd imagine opens up. The thick bare-concrete pillars are striking, the ceilings are high, and the atmosphere is open. Like a café — no, even more contemporary, yet a mysterious space with a faint scent of the Ryukyus. 🪟Through the big windows you can see Okinawa's nature. Waza-ya, you see, has set up shop in a corner of this space. In front, a banner reading "The Original Ryukyu Suba Waza-ya" flutters prominently. Ordering is by ticket machine, and there's a really extensive lineup, from Okinawa soba–related items to Taiwanese ramen, and even gyoza and rice dishes.

Well, here comes the "soy sauce ramen". 😍The soup seems to be based on rich pork bone, kombu, and bonito broth. But I couldn't even tell if it was soy sauce flavored — it's somehow complex, with a pretty rich, heavy taste. 🍲What I'm most curious about, you see, is this soup. Is it an Okinawa soba–style broth, or authentic ramen? The line between them is blurry. It's distinctive, but feels like something different from regular Okinawa soba.

The biggest highlight, you see, is these noodles! 🤩They use medium-thick Okinawa soba noodles. Just from looking at them, you see, you can tell they're chewy. When you actually eat them, you see, they have the springy texture of fresh noodles. The noodles alone are completely Okinawa soba. This part is really well done. The toppings are basically ramen 😅 A big slice of chashu sits there prominently, and there's a soft-boiled egg on top too.

The lu rou fan, in the tradition of Taiwanese ramen, is pork simmered down in a miso flavor, piled generously over white rice. There's mitsuba on top too. This lu rou fan also has a pretty robust seasoning, and eating it together with the ramen boosts the satisfaction in one go. Overall the volume is more than enough. There's so much that I got a little worried whether I could finish it all by myself.

If I were to describe Waza-ya in one phrase, you see, it'd be "ramen made with Okinawa soba noodles". It's not completely Okinawa soba, but it's not completely ramen either. If anything, maybe it's more like "Okinawa soba in the shape of ramen". The banner saying "Ryukyu suba" also conveys that spirit of challenge. 😄It's precisely because the location, you see, is inside a cultural facility called the National Theatre Okinawa that a concept like this "fusion of tradition and novelty" might be so naturally accepted. It has an atmosphere where not only tourists but locals too can drop by casually.

Rating (9/15): It's ramen with Okinawa soba noodles (^0^;) Simply tasty.
