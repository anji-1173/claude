Maruchi Chatan-ten

まるち 北谷店
Yoshihara, Chatan TownOld Okinawan houseRating 9/15

Maruchi's third shop, marked by white Ryukyu roof tiles and a shisa. So popular a line forms right at opening; the customization of 3 soups × 3 noodles is the same, and there's also bone-in pork rib (hon-soki) soba exclusive to the Chatan branch. Lunch comes with complimentary jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice).

On my Okinawa soba tour, this time I visited “Maruchi Chatan-ten,” an Okinawa soba specialty shop in Yoshihara, Chatan Town 🍜　I ate here in March 2025 😄

My first encounter with “Maruchi” was the Maruchi Nakagusuku branch, and after that I also visited the Nanjo City Tsukishiro branch, so this time is actually my visit to a third branch.

The Nakagusuku branch and the Nanjo City branch were both popular, and this Chatan branch is no exception: customers were lining up in droves right at opening 😲

Maybe because it's in Chatan Town, an area with lots of tourists, it seems to draw a wide range of customers from locals to tourists, and I was reminded again of how popular the Maruchi brand is.

On the exterior, the roof of white Ryukyu tiles, so typical of Okinawa, stands out against the blue sky, and a shisa welcomes you from on top of the roof 🦁　Not only inside, but picnic tables are also lined up outdoors on the gravel, and on a nice day it looks pleasant to eat outside ✨　It's an old-folk-house-style building, but the inside has been neatly renovated, and it gave a calm yet clean impression.

Now, looking at the menu, a big feature of Maruchi is the system where you can choose the soup and the noodles each from 3 kinds. The soup is a choice of 3: “light soup (our secret recipe),” “rich soup (our pride),” and “salt soup (simple is best).” My experience with the light soup at the Nakagusuku branch had stuck in my memory, so this time I tried choosing the “rich soup” 💪　It's apparently a rich preparation made by simmering prefectural chicken and pork bone for a long time, which raises my expectations 🤤　You can choose the noodles from “thin noodles,” “flat noodles,” and “wavy noodles,” and this time I went with the staff's recommendation and chose “wavy noodles.”

What I ordered this time was the Chatan-branch-exclusive menu item “bone-in pork rib (hon-soki) soba (large) ¥980.” It coming with the lunchtime-service jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice) was a nice point too 🍚

Facing the bowl that was brought out, first a sip of the soup. As expected of the rich type, the chicken and pork bone broth flavor is solid. However, the saltiness is a bit strong, and I got a somewhat different impression from the creamy, mellow smoothness of the pork-bone-based soups at Nankuru, Uehara Soba, and Tsukikuroki. Rather than a deep umami, the salt comes to the front 🤔

As for the wavy noodles, they're a medium-thick type with a moderate chewiness, and they cling to the soup quite well too 😊

The topping, the bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), are well simmered and melt-in-your-mouth tender overall, but depending on the part, there were places where a sinewy texture remained, giving the impression of a mix of tender parts and parts with a solid meaty feel 😄　And the jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice) that came as a lunchtime-only service is a delight! The combination of Okinawa soba and jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice) really is the best ✨

Rating (9/15): Comparing Maruchi's three branches, the Nakagusuku branch, the Nanjo City Tsukishiro branch, and the Chatan branch, you can see they all have steady popularity. The atmosphere created by making use of old folk houses and the high degree of customization, where you can choose the soup and noodles, must be what attracts so many customers. The taste is “just plain delicious,” and my impression is that rather than having a standout personality, it's an Okinawa soba you can eat with peace of mind. Even so, I think the brand power that creates a line right after opening is the real thing ✨
