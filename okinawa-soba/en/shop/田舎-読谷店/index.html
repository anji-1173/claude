Inaka Yomitan-ten

A Miyako soba and soki soba specialty shop in Uechi, Yomitan Village. With outstanding value at ¥550 for Miyako soba, it's the traditional Miyako soba style where lean pork hides under the noodles. “Inaka Yomitan-ten” in Uechi, Yomitan Village. I went to eat here in August 2024 😄

The shop's exterior truly has the feel of the “countryside” (inaka). The sign at the entrance has “Inaka” written in big letters, promoting “soki soba.” There's also an old vending machine on the premises, giving it a somehow nostalgic atmosphere.

When you go inside, a plain interior spreads out before you, with wooden tables and chairs lined up reminiscent of an old-fashioned Okinawan shokudo. There are several notices on the walls, and the one that catches the eye is the notice “Due to a pork shortage, the number of soki pieces has been reduced.” You can tell they're making all sorts of efforts to maintain cost performance.

Prices have been going up and up recently, but it's nice that at this shop you can enjoy delicious soba at the reasonable price of ¥550. The “soki soba” is offered at the same price too, so I think it's a shop that's quite easy on the wallet.

From the menu, I chose “Miyako soba” 😆

The “Miyako soba” has been brought out. The first thing that jumped out at me was its simple visual. Thin noodles are neatly arranged in a pale soup, with just two round fish cakes (kamaboko) on top. At a glance it seems there are few toppings, but actually the lean pork hidden under the noodles is the tradition of Miyako soba 😄

First, when I take a sip of the soup, the pork bone flavor spreads through my whole mouth, and within it I can sense a faint sweetness. I think this soup has a taste different from other Miyako soba. Although it's a pork-bone-based soup, it's characterized by a somehow gentle taste.

The noodles are thin and straight, soft yet with chewiness / bite, and they go perfectly with the soup. The lean pork is a bit firm and a sinewy texture remains, but you can enjoy how the flavor increases each time you chew it.

“Inaka Yomitan-ten,” which serves soba of this quality at a price of ¥550, has unbeatable cost performance. I think it's the perfect shop for when you want to enjoy delicious Okinawa soba quickly. That said, if you're looking for something special, it might be a bit lacking. But I think the appeal of this shop is, above all, its plainness and nostalgia. It makes you feel as if you've slipped back in time into an old Okinawan scene.

Rating (9/15): This time's “Miyako soba” gets a rating of ☆3, with the soup, noodles, and toppings all feeling ordinary. It's by no means bad; rather, I think it's a dish you can be satisfied with, given this taste at this price range. It reminded me of “Takoyaki Champon” in Okinawa City, which also has outstanding cost performance. A meal at this shop isn't especially luxurious, but I think it makes you feel a warmth that warms your heart. It's the kind of place I'd like to casually drop by again 😊
