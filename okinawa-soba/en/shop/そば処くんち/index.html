Soba-dokoro Kunchi

そば処くんち
Shuri Torihori-cho, NahaRating 9/15

An Okinawa soba shop in Shuri Torihori-cho, Naha, near Shuri Station on the monorail. A pork-bone-based soup with soft, thin straight noodles. The "Kunchi Soba" (¥900) comes topped with firmly seasoned bone-in pork ribs and braised pork belly. Sanshin are lined up inside the shop, and you may get to hear the owner sing with the sanshin.

On this Okinawa soba journey, I visited "Soba-dokoro Kunchi" in Shuri, Naha City 🍜 I ate here in July 2025 😄

It's a shop in a building along the street, near Shuri Station on the monorail.

Above the entrance is a "Soba-dokoro Kunchi" sign, and beside it hangs a sign with a photo of soba, so you can tell right away this is an Okinawa soba shop 😊

You order at the ticket machine at the entrance. The signature Kunchi Soba is ¥900, and the set meal is ¥1,100. Others lined up include Okinawa soba, soki soba, tebichi soba, soft-bone pork rib soba, and jushi set meals.

The interior has the feel of an old-fashioned diner, with a TV and noren curtains hanging. The only customers that day were a local student and me.

What I ordered this time was the "Kunchi Soba," named after the shop. A bowl topped with bone-in pork ribs and braised pork belly ✨

First, the soup. It has a pork-bone-based flavor 😋

The noodles are thin and straight, boiled on the soft side.

The bone-in pork ribs and braised pork belly toppings are both firmly seasoned and the type with a solid bite. The sinewy texture was clearly there too ^^;

And from here is what surprised me most at this shop. I'd been thinking there were quite a few sanshin lined up in one corner of the shop, when suddenly the owner performed singing with the sanshin for me 😅 I got to hear Asadoya Yunta and a few other songs 😅

Here's how it looked 🎶 Live music, unexpectedly, in the middle of a diner.

On the wall are posted a "list of famous Ryukyuan sanshin," a newspaper clipping saying "the owner's sanshin performances are popular," and lots of photos of him holding a sanshin, so this is the shop's specialty. Since it's close to Shuri Station, it may be a welcome service for those visiting as tourists 😄

Rating (9/15): A pork-bone-based soup with soft, thin straight noodles. The bone-in pork ribs and braised pork belly were firmly seasoned, with a chewy, sinewy texture. My impression of the flavor itself is that it's average, but it was a shop where you can have an experience you can only get here: the owner's singing with the sanshin 😊
