Igei Service Area (Northbound) Restaurant

伊芸サービスエリア（下り）レストラン
Igei, Kin Town (Igei SA, Northbound)Rating 9/15

A restaurant in the Igei Service Area (northbound) on the Okinawa Expressway. With its red tile roof and shisa, it has an Okinawan exterior, and serves a classic old-fashioned Okinawa soba topped with green onions, shredded egg, fish cake, and braised pork belly. A reassuring bowl for a break in your travels.

I ate here in March 2025 😊

This time, while driving on the Okinawa Expressway, I stopped at the Igei Service Area (northbound) and had “Okinawa soba” 🍜

When going around eating Okinawa soba, your eyes tend to go to famous shops aimed at tourists or traditional neighborhood diners, but Okinawa soba at service areas like this also catches my interest, since you get to see the “everyday bowl” of Okinawa. The exterior's red tiles catch the eye, giving the feel of “oh, this is Okinawan” at first glance 😆 The shisa on the roof add good flavor too, and it was just the right atmosphere for taking a little break during a trip.

Inside is a spacious area typical of a service area, with a simple diner-like quality, and the lack of pretension was oddly calming. In front of the ticket machine I hesitated a little over what to eat, but this time I chose the basic “Okinawa soba” after all.

The moment I saw the actual bowl, I thought it was truly a traditional, standard style. In a dark-colored bowl sat green onions, shredded egg, fish cake (kamaboko), and braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) — a classic style without gimmicks just by looking at it. With this kind of bowl you can somehow imagine the direction of the flavor before eating, but that “reassurance of it being just as you imagined” is also the good thing about Okinawa soba 😊 First, I took a sip of the broth and it was the type with a pork-bone-based richness. However, rather than being too thick or punchy, my impression was that it was clearly put together as a bowl to eat at a service area. It's not bad, but it didn't make me excited with an “Oh, this is delicious!” — it was a pretty ordinary broth.

The noodles were medium-thick, loose straight noodles with a definite Okinawa soba feel. Lifted up, they also looked just like diner Okinawa soba. But when I ate them they were a bit on the soft side. Personally I would have liked a bit more springiness and bite.

Among the toppings, what left the strongest impression was the braised pork belly, as expected. It was clearly well seasoned just from looking at it. However, there was also a little sinewy texture, and if you expect a softness that gently unravels in your mouth, it's a bit different. It's properly simmered, but not the melt-in-your-mouth type. So for people seeking strong satisfaction from the braised pork belly, it might need one more step. The fish cake, green onions, and shredded egg were the reliable supporting players, tidying up the overall look while properly supporting the old-fashioned Okinawa soba feel.

What I thought while eating this time was that this bowl works as a “reassuring Okinawa soba to eat along the way on a trip,” but if you ask whether I'd stop in just for this, it doesn't quite get there. Comparing within the Okinawa Expressway, I thought the Okinawa soba I ate earlier at “Ajisen Ramen” in the Nakagusuku Service Area was more delicious. The Okinawa soba at this Igei Service Area (northbound) was “ordinary,” in both the good and bad senses. Since I always look for each shop's individuality and a memorable point, in that sense I felt a bit of dissatisfaction.

Still, being able to casually eat this classic-style Okinawa soba at a service area diner is very Okinawan and nice. During sightseeing, between travel legs, when you're a little hungry and don't want to take any adventure but want Okinawa soba, I think it's perfectly fine. It's not flashy, but it felt like they serve a traditional standard Okinawa soba just as it is.

Rating (9/15): My honest take is “ordinary, not exceeding expectations; not so good that I actively want to repeat it” 🙂 But why not try it once when sightseeing in the north? 😆
