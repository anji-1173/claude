Chatan Soba

北谷そば
Kuwae, Chatan TownRating 9/15

A popular shop that uses Chatan salt (100% seawater from off the Miyagi coast). The wall covered in autograph boards is overwhelming, and the signature dish is the seared salt nankotsu soba. You can choose from medium-flat noodles or wavy noodles.

This time my Okinawa soba tour took me to “Chatan Soba” in Kuwae, Chatan Town 🍜　I ate here in March 2025 😄

The exterior is a bit unique; it's in a corner of an apartment building, so to speak, with the shop's name written big on the wooden enclosure below the building. White stones are laid around the entrance, along with wooden decorations and handwritten signs, and the contrast with the concrete feel outside was interesting. There was also a notice for the “tofu soba,” limited to 10 servings, and this kind of handmade-feeling presentation felt somehow friendly 😊

When you go inside, this is quite distinctive too: autograph boards are lined up across an entire wall, making a strong impact. There were autographs from players of the Kings and the Chunichi Dragons too, a space where you immediately understand, “Ah, this place is pretty famous.” There are also tatami seats with the warmth of wood, and including the lighting and decorations, it had a calm, Okinawan atmosphere overall, and I felt that rather than being aimed at tourists, it must be a shop properly loved by local customers too. However, there were actually lots of local customers and it seems quite popular, so on this day I waited a fair while. Moreover, even after being shown to my seat, the food didn't come out quickly from there, and overall the wait was on the long side. Even if not as long as at Jagaru Soba, it might feel a bit long to impatient people (^0^;)

Looking at the menu, there were Okinawa soba, soki soba, salt nankotsu soba, mixed soba, and so on, and the way they push Chatan salt was memorable too. There was also a notice that they use “Chatan salt” made from 100% seawater from off the Miyagi coast in Chatan Town, which clearly conveyed their commitment to ingredients. This time I ordered the recommended “seared salt nankotsu soba,” and since I could choose the noodles from medium-flat noodles or wavy noodles, I chose the wavy noodles, which were also recommended. Being able to choose your own noodles is a nice little point.

The bowl that was brought out looked fairly simple. In a clear soup sat seared soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), with green onions and omelet alongside. When I first drank the soup, it was pork-bone-based, yet the salt came clearly to the front. As you'd expect from using Chatan salt, it has personality, but personally I felt the saltiness was a bit strong. It's a light type, but rather than gently and slowly soaking in, it was a type of soup that pretty clearly tells you, “The salt is coming through.”

The medium-thin wavy noodles I chose were on the soft side in texture, and people who want to fully enjoy firmness might find them a bit lacking.

The seared salt soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki) have a browned look that whets the appetite, and when I actually ate them, overall they leaned light, with a melt-in-your-mouth tenderness too. However, depending on the part, there were places where a sinewy texture clearly remained, so not all of it was the melting type. I thought the toastiness of the searing would come forward more, but that was surprisingly modest, and my impression was that, for better or worse, it came together safely. The composition of the toppings is simple too, so rather than focusing on impact, it's a calm bowl.

Looking at it as a whole, it was a shop with lots of intriguing elements, like the shop's atmosphere, the commitment to ingredients, its local popularity, and the number of autographs, and I think it actually has an appeal that makes you want to try it once. However, speaking honestly as Anji, compared with how my expectations rose, including the wait, the taste landed at “ordinary.” I didn't feel a strong deciding factor like “I want to come back to eat this again!”

Rating (9/15): It seems to be a popular shop, but my honest impression was that the taste is just plain delicious, I guess~. It's worth experiencing once including the atmosphere, but the decisive factor that makes you want to come back is weak; that's the kind of bowl it was 🙂
