Mendokoro Mannen

I went to eat here in November 2024 😄

I have the goal of touring every Okinawa soba shop on the main island of Okinawa, and I'm eating my way through little by little. I'm still far from conquering every shop, but this time I'll introduce “Mendokoro Mannen” in Nakanishi, Urasoe City 😊

“Mendokoro Mannen” is located a little way in from National Route 58 in Nakanishi, Urasoe City. The exterior is an ordinary soba shop, but when I went inside and looked at the menu, I noticed it has a feature different from other Okinawa soba shops. Believe it or not, like a ramen shop, you can choose from 3 kinds of soup: “salt,” “soy sauce,” and “miso”! 😲

This is interesting. I'd previously eaten Okinawa soba with miso soup at “Nagomi” in Nago City, so I was going to go with miso soup this time too, but a staff member told me “The salt soup is recommended,” so I obediently switched to salt soup.

Looking at the menu, in addition to the signature “Mannen soba,” there's also “vegetable soba.” It seems you can choose salt, soy sauce, or miso for both. Moreover, at the top of the menu there was an explanation that they had changed from pork belly to chicken breast. Pork is the standard for Okinawa soba, but chicken is an interesting new attempt too 🐔

Inside, there are counter seats and table seats; it's simple but clean. On the tables were condiments such as house-made kōrēgūsu (chili-infused awamori) and shichimi.

The “Mannen soba (salt)” I ordered has arrived! Visually, there's chicken breast, chopped green onions, and the distinctive red fish cake (kamaboko), vivid in a clear soup 😄

First, a sip of the soup. Hm? This tastes more like ramen soup than Okinawa soba! It's a salt soup based on chicken broth, and I can taste the flavor of sesame oil too. There's not much of the pork bone flavor typical of Okinawa soba, but this is delicious in its own way 😋

Next, I have the noodles. Medium-thin noodles with a loose wave, a smooth texture with a slight chewiness, and I wondered, are these house-made fresh noodles? They go well with the soup and cling to it properly.

The topping, the soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), has a light seasoning and is simmered melt-in-your-mouth tender. The chicken breast, which replaced the pork belly, also surprisingly suits this soup and didn't feel out of place. The combination of the salt soup's refreshing taste and chicken actually feels natural.

What I suddenly thought after finishing was, “Next time I'd like to try the miso soup too.” I got curious, wondering what Okinawa soba with miso soup tastes like.

I felt that “Mendokoro Mannen” is a shop that, while protecting the tradition of Okinawa soba, is pursuing new possibilities for Okinawa soba by adding the options of salt, soy sauce, and miso. It's a creative type of Okinawa soba, but I think it's a dish worth trying at least once 😊

Rating (10.5/15): An Okinawa soba shop with distinctive soups you can choose from salt, soy sauce, or miso. This time's salt soup tasted more like ramen than Okinawa soba, but I enjoyed it as delicious in its own way. The noodles were smooth with a moderate chewiness, and the toppings were cooked tender too. It's recommended for people who want to enjoy a new variation of Okinawa soba!
