Izumi Soba

I went to eat here in February 2025 😁😊

For this Okinawa soba outing, I went to “Izumi Soba” in Izumi, Motobu Town 😄

This shop is near “Mori no Ie,” where I ate the other day, and is also in the mountains 🌿 Following the navigation down a narrow road, an old house suddenly appears alone, surrounded by trees. A big sign with the words “house-made noodles” stands out, greeting you with a red tile roof and a shisa perched on it ✨

When I arrived, there was a “Full” sign at the entrance...! As expected, it's a popular shop. A waiting tent was set up outside, and the system was to wait your turn there. On the sign-in stand were paper and a pen, and you write your name and wait to be called 📝

I waited quite a while that day~ When there's a line, expectations rise too 😆

After waiting a while I finally got inside. There's a ticket machine right at the entrance, so it's a style where you buy a ticket here. You take off your shoes and step up, and the wooden warmth inside is new and clean 🏠 It has the feel of a renovated old house, with exposed ceiling beams giving an open feeling. There was also a sign on the wall reading “Sold out for today,” so it seems it sometimes sells out early.

When I sat down, the view out the window was a green landscape with luxuriant hego tree ferns, truly giving the feeling of “eating in the Yanbaru forest” 🌴 The atmosphere is outstanding.

Looking at the menu, the “Izumi Soba,” which shares the shop's name, was written as the shop's popular No. 1. It has two kinds of meat, bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki) and braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), plus 2 small side dishes, so it's a good-value menu. They also had “soki soba,” “braised pork belly soba,” and a rare limited-quantity item called “Tojin Soba.” Tojin Soba recreates the origin of Okinawa soba from about 120 years ago, and is apparently a black soy-sauce-based broth 🍜

Naturally this time I decided to order the “Izumi Soba” (¥950), which shares the shop's name.

After waiting a few minutes, the Izumi Soba was brought out on a tray! Beside the soba come pickled bitter melon (goya) and 2 small bowls of tofu. It's full of Okinawan flavor just by looking at it 😊

First, as always, I start with the broth. When I took a sip, it was a gentle seasoning with the bonito stock coming through firmly.

Next, the noodles. I had been expecting them since they're house-made, and they're medium-thick wavy noodles with a chewy quality 🍜 When you bite, there's a firm springiness, and they cling nicely to the broth. These noodles are quite delicious.

I tried the toppings of bone-in pork ribs and braised pork belly. The seasoning is moderate and not bad, but there's quite a lot of sinewy texture. My impression was that I'd have been happier if they had been simmered a bit more tenderly. The fish cake and green onions were also properly included.

The small side dish of pickled bitter melon has a firm bitterness, and it's surprisingly delicious 😋 It's perfect as a palate cleanser and went well with the soba.

Rating (9.5/15): As an overall impression, because there was a line and I waited quite a while, my expectations had risen, but honestly it felt like it was one step short~ The atmosphere of an old house in the mountains is outstanding. The chewiness of the house-made noodles was good, but the sinewy texture of the toppings bothered me a little. It's a just plain tasty Okinawa soba 😊 Being an old house, the interior is new and clean, the green view from the window is superb, and I'm sure it's a popular shop ✨ I recommend it to those who want to enjoy the atmosphere and savor Okinawa soba slowly.

It might be good to stop by on the way toward Motobu Town, especially to the Churaumi Aquarium or Jungle-ia Okinawa. However, I recommend going at an early time of day, prepared for a line 😄
