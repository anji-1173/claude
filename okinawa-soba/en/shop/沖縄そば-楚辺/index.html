Okinawa Soba Sobe

沖縄そば 楚辺
Sobe, NahaRating 9/15

A hugely popular shop with a striking old-folk-house-style building surrounded by trees. In the spacious interior lined with surfboards and celebrity autographs, the specialty is the “Three-Star Makanai Soba Set Meal” (with braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), and bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki)), limited to 30 servings a day.

I went to eat here in March 2025 😄 This time my Okinawa soba tour brings me to “Okinawa Soba Sobe” in Sobe, Naha City. The moment I arrived in front of the shop, my first impression was “Whoa, this place has a strong vibe.”

An entrance like an old folk house surrounded by trees, and a sign full of character. Even though it's in town, the air feels a little more relaxed just there, and I totally get why people who come sightseeing get excited. In fact, the guide signs were conspicuous too, and even before going inside, there was a clear sense of “this must be a popular shop.”

Inside, the spacious interior had surfboards on display and lots of celebrity autographs, and it was a very memorable space where Okinawan clutter mixed with touristy glamour 😊

What I had this time was the “Three-Star Makanai Soba Set Meal,” limited to 30 servings a day. The name alone has a bit of a special feel, and since it's topped with three kinds, braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), and bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), it's visually very satisfying. In fact, when it was brought out the bowl was quite luxurious, and I thought, “This is the kind that gets my hopes up.” The set meal comes with jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice), a small side dish, and even mozuku in vinegar, so I think it's a welcome lineup for tourists in terms of volume too. You can taste various aspects of Okinawa soba in one set meal, so it's an easy-to-understand set for first-timers.

First, the soup: among combined broths, it's a type where the pork bone's presence comes a bit to the front. It's just plain delicious, but it doesn't quite reach a personality that sticks strongly in your memory; that was my impression.

The noodles are thin, straight, and on the soft side. People who want to enjoy solid firmness and a wheaty feel might find them a bit lacking.

The toppings are braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), and bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), a lineup that gets you pretty excited just looking at the names. It had volume visually too, and a glamour worthy of the name “Three-Star.” But when I ate it, overall it leaned light, and I was bothered that each had a little sinewy texture remaining. If you're going to enjoy three kinds of meat, I'd have been happy if they were a bit more melt-in-your-mouth, or if the differences between each cut came through more clearly.

On the other hand, what was good were the small side dishes rounding out the set meal. The mozuku in vinegar was refreshing and felt great for resetting my palate. The bean sprout namul quietly did a good job too, and when small dishes like these are properly delicious, the satisfaction of the whole set meal goes up. The jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice) was also on the light side, a balance that doesn't get in the way of the soba's taste. This might be why it's supported by tourists and a wide range of ages.

However, I want to be honest here. The shop's atmosphere, its size, the building with a sense of history, the number of autographs, the number of tourists, even the special feel of the limited menu, all of it was there, so my expectations had gone up quite a bit. But when I actually finished eating, “just plain delicious” was my most honest impression 😅 It's not bad at all, and it's properly delicious. But it's also a fact that in Naha and Okinawa there are shops that grab you more with “the power of the soba itself.” That's exactly why I thought the reason so many people gather at this shop isn't just the taste, but must largely be its appeal including the strength of word of mouth, the shop's history, its location that's easy to visit while sightseeing, and that atmosphere.

Rating (9/15): I think it suits people who want to enjoy Okinawa soba leisurely in a spacious interior while feeling the Okinawan atmosphere on their trip. On the other hand, for people who “just want to be wowed by the taste of the soba alone,” I felt there's a chance it might be a bit of a letdown. It's worth going once including the atmosphere, but it wasn't a bowl that made me strongly feel I absolutely want to eat it again when I revisit. Still, actually going to a popular shop like this and confirming with my own tongue “so this is the type it is” is also part of the fun of an Okinawa soba tour 🌺
