Waramaru Shoten

わらまる商店
Osato, Nanjo CityRating 9/15

A shop that sells itself on being additive-free. An additive-free broth of Makurazaki-grown flower bonito and Hokkaido kelp with sun-dried salt, and plump fresh noodles aged 24 hours. The Kinnikuman-style illustrations on the wall are fun too. A shop featured in Ageage Meshi.

* This article was created with AI (Chat GPT 4o) and partly supplemented by me 😁

I ate here in November 2024 😆

I visited a stamp rally participating shop for the first time in a while 😊

This time I visited “Waramaru Shoten” in Osato, Nanjo City.

It’s a calm-looking shop on the first floor of an apartment building, and the hand-drawn sign and noren reading “Okinawa soba, additive-free” are its landmarks 🏮

At the entrance is displayed the phrase “Open and going strong today!”, conveying a lively atmosphere ✨

First I bought a “Waramaru Soba (large)” for ¥1,150 at the ticket machine. The machine also says “Our noodles are fresh noodles,” which raises expectations 🍜

After a while the “Waramaru Soba” was brought out, and it exudes gentleness just by its looks 😊

The broth is, I was told, an additive-free stock combining flower bonito from Makurazaki, Kagoshima Prefecture and kelp from Hokkaido, with sun-dried salt added. When you take a sip, the umami spreads gently and soaks into your body. It’s very light, and you can enjoy a natural flavor 🌿

The noodles are medium-thin, slightly wavy, aged fresh noodles. Rested for 24 hours or more, these noodles have a plump and slurpy texture with a nice feel going down, and they go well with the broth ✨

The toppings are braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), fish cake (kamaboko), shredded egg, yushi-dofu, and chopped green onions.

The braised pork belly and soft-bone pork ribs are lightly seasoned and both are simmered until melt-in-your-mouth tender, though with a faint sinewy texture. The yushi-dofu adds mellowness and ties everything together gently 😊

As I ate on, I enjoyed changing the flavor with pickled red ginger, shichimi, and kōrēgūsu (chili-infused awamori) 🌶️

The spicy kick of kōrēgūsu goes well with the light broth, and the pickled red ginger makes a nice accent too 😉

On the walls inside the shop are hand-drawn Kinnikuman-style illustrations, making it a fun space with a bit of playfulness 🎨 There were also notices that convey the shop’s sincerity, such as their commitment to being additive-free, the use of rice oil and sesame oil for cooking oil, and the use of soft water 💛

Lastly, I made sure to get the stamp rally stamp 📸♪

Overall, the broth had more flavor than Shuri-style Okinawa soba, yet its light and gentle taste was memorable. Personally, though, I’d be happier with a bit more punch. Even so, it’s a bowl in which you can feel the commitment to ingredients and a body-friendly finish 😊

Rating (9/15): It’s just plain tasty. When you come to Nanjo City, why not give it a try once?
