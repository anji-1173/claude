Chikin no Maruyaki Kokkoroko

チキンの丸焼きこっころこ
Awase, Okinawa CityUniqueRating 9/15

A one-of-a-kind chicken soba made by a whole-roast-chicken specialty shop. Garlicky roast chicken sits boldly on top, and the soup, infused with the fat and aroma of the roast, has a character no other shop has. Keihan (chicken rice) and takeout whole roast chicken too.

I ate here in December 2024 😄 Today I visited "Chikin no Maruyaki Kokkoroko" in Awase, Okinawa City.

The sign and exterior are cute, with wood-grain accents standing out on the white building along the road 🏠 There's a ramp in front of the shop, too, giving it an easy-to-enter feel.

When you go inside, the first thing that jumps out at you is the wall of autograph boards above the counter 📸 You can feel how loved it is by locals. When you sit down, the savory smell of roast chicken is already gently tickling your nose, and your appetite switch turns on naturally 😄

Flip through the menu and, as expected, the star is chicken. A whole roast is ¥2,000, the "tori-zara (half)" to enjoy in the shop is ¥1,200, and the quarter (Kokkoroko Special) is ¥1,000 — a solid lineup. For takeout fans there's also the "Chicken L pack (1/2) ¥1,200" and "S pack (1/4) ¥850", so munching on it at home while watching a movie sounds nice too 🚗 I also discovered a new menu item, "keihan (chicken rice) ¥800". It seems to be an arrangement of the Amami Oshima local dish using their roast chicken, so it's decided as homework for next time 🍗✨

That said, today I came to eat Okinawa soba, so I ordered the "chicken soba" in "large".

The bowl that arrived has, as in the photo, roast chicken sitting boldly on top of the soup. The skin is nicely browned, and from the first bite the garlic in the seasoning comes through strongly, with an impact that makes you go "Oh!" 🧄🍜 The clear golden soup is less the sharp, "straight-up clear chicken broth" type and more the type where the fat and aroma of the roast have slowly melted in. Rather than competing on the outline of the broth alone, it pushes forward with the savoriness only a chicken specialty shop can offer. A flavor you can only find here — I kept nodding and my spoon wouldn't stop 😋

The noodles are medium-thick and gently wavy. Pale and smooth, cooked on the soft side. Rather than pulling you along with chewiness, they're designed to coat themselves in soup as you slurp. Personally I'd like a bit more bite, but they have a "good receiving quality" that catches the punch of the star chicken and garlic, so the overall flow isn't bad. When you lift the noodles, the aroma spreads softly with the steam, and adding a little green onion lightens the aftertaste. Adding pickled red ginger midway tightens everything up at once, and the heaviness of the second half disappears — that's nice too 🥢

And then the chicken topping. When you put your chopsticks in, it comes apart tenderly along the fibers. The skin is savory, and the inside is moist. As the garlic seasoning blends into the soup, its sharp edges soften and layers of umami build up, which is fun. It's nice that you can reset your palate with the namasu-style side dish and go back to the soup in a loop. Since I went with "large", it's more filling than it looks, and my stomach was well satisfied 🐔

I think what makes this bowl interesting is that it's complete not through "purity of broth" but through "the shop's character". The worldview is clear with three points: the roast aroma, the garlic flavor, and the soft noodles, and if you come expecting the classic pork-bone or bonito-forward Okinawa soba, you'll be betrayed in a good way. Opinions may be divided, but if asked "Can you only eat this here?", I'd proudly answer yes. It hits the spot on days when you want to eat heartily or recharge your stamina after work. If you go along mixing in flavor changes with the pickled red ginger and green onion on the table, you can enjoy it without getting bored until the end. For those who like spicy food, next time I'm curious about the chili sauce suggestion for the "keihan" too 🌶️

A price revision notice was posted in the shop, but I felt a determination of "we'll satisfy you with the volume on the plate" to make up for it.

They also offer takeout and vacuum packs, so it seems handy for family gatherings and as a treat to bring to people 📝 Making "our own chicken soba" at home by preparing a hot soup and topping it with a quarter sounds fun too. There are days for tackling the classic tourist spots, and days like this for dropping casually by a distinctive local shop, and I reflected that both are good ✨

Rating (9/15): All in all, the chicken soba at "Chikin no Maruyaki Kokkoroko" was a bowl that "pulls you along with the deliciousness of the meat", lightly leaping beyond the framework of Okinawa soba. The direction of the soup isn't the easy-to-grasp type, but the savoriness of the roast and the punch of garlic keep you eating right to the end. If you're looking for something distinctive, or it's a day you want to eat well, please give it a try 🍜
