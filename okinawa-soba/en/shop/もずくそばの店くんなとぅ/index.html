Mozuku Soba Kunnatou

もずくそばの店くんなとぅ
Tamagusuku Shikenbaru, Nanjo CityRating 9/15

A mozuku seaweed soba shop by the sea in Tamagusuku Shikenbaru, Nanjo City. House-made thick flat noodles kneaded with mozuku, in a blended broth on the salty side. The "Mozuku Soba (sanmai-niku)" (large ¥1,060) comes with fresh mozuku, mozuku in vinegar and mozuku jelly, and refills of fresh mozuku are free. At the outdoor tables you can eat while looking out at the sea.

On my Okinawa soba journey, this time I visited "Mozuku Soba Kunnatou" in Tamagusuku, Nanjo City 🍜 I ate here in July 2025 😄

It's a shop by the sea, with trees growing thick above a stone wall.

"House-made noodles — Mozuku Soba Shop Kunnatou" is written in big letters on the stone wall, and that's the landmark.

At the entrance there's a wooden board with "Kunnatou" written on it, and a blue noren (shop curtain) reading "Specialty: Mozuku Soba." At your feet, tanuki (raccoon dog) figurines welcome you 😊

Inside, fishing nets are stretched across the ceiling, giving it the feel of a shop near the sea. A big banner reading "Mozuku Soba Kunnatou" hangs on the wall too. Tourists came in right as the shop opened, and it was lively.

Above the window there's a long row of autograph boards. On the shelf above them, bottles of awamori are lined up as well.

The menu is mozuku all the way. Mozuku Soba (sanmai-niku) comes in three sizes — small ¥690, medium ¥830, large ¥1,060 — and the single-item soba dishes come with fresh mozuku, mozuku in vinegar and mozuku jelly. There are also Mozuku Soki Soba, Mozuku Nakami Soba (pork offal), Mozuku Vegetable Soba, Mozuku Zaru Soba (cold), and set meals that come with jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice) and mozuku tempura.

And the nice part is "free refills of fresh mozuku." The notice says to use the small dish that comes with your meal and help yourself from the refrigerator in front of the register ✨

This time I ordered the "Mozuku Soba (sanmai-niku)," with braised pork belly. The rim of the bowl has "Kunnatou" written on it too. Fresh mozuku, mozuku in vinegar and mozuku jelly were brought out with it.

First, the soup. It's a blended broth, but honestly I couldn't tell which ingredient stood out. It is on the salty side, though.

Inside the refrigerator there's plenty of "organic fresh mozuku." You take some onto your small dish from here and add it to your soba.

The noodles are house-made with mozuku kneaded in, but I couldn't taste the mozuku ^^; They are thick flat noodles, cooked on the soft side.

Eating the fresh mozuku together with the noodles eases the heat of the soba, and it's nice that it's healthy too 😋

The sanmai-niku topping is well seasoned all the way through. That said, it did have a sinewy texture ^^;

There's also a retail corner inside, with dried "Mozuku-men" noodles (thin and thick, ¥600 each), dried mozuku and more on display. Notices such as "Japan's first organic mozuku" and "Made with Okinawan mozuku we produce ourselves" were posted too, and you can feel how serious they are about mozuku.

And the charm of this shop is its location. At the outdoor tables, you can eat while looking out at the sea 🌊

Rating (9/15): A blended broth on the salty side, with soft, thick flat noodles kneaded with mozuku. The sanmai-niku was well seasoned all the way through, but had a sinewy texture. Eating the free-refill fresh mozuku together with the noodles eases the heat and is healthy, which is nice. The taste is good in an ordinary way, I'd say. It was a shop with a great location, where you can eat while looking out at the sea 😊
