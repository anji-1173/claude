Papaya to Subui

パパイヤとスブイ
Oyama, Ginowan (inside Ginowan Yui Marche)UniqueRating 10.5/15

Creative Okinawa soba with distinctive shiitake broth and papaya and subui (winter melon) toppings. After Ishikawa in Uruma City and Matsuo in Naha City, it now operates inside Ginowan Yui Marche. The Lemosco condiment is something other shops don't have.

― 3rd visit (May 2025, Ginowan Yui Marche) ―

I ate here in May 2025 😆 This time on my Okinawa soba tour, I went to "Ginowan Yui Marche" in Oyama, Ginowan City.

Inside the marche, lined with local vegetables and souvenirs, is "Papaya to Subui no Okinawa Soba". From outside, the bright yellow-green sign catches your eye, and it has a somewhat quirky atmosphere — you'd never think there was an Okinawa soba shop inside a marche.

I'd eaten at Papasubu before at the Uruma City location and the location in front of the Prefectural Office, but this was my first visit in quite a long time. There's a ticket machine at the front, and it's lined with Okinawa soba you rarely see elsewhere, starting with Papasubu soba, plus sunui shiitake soba, shikuwasa wasabi soba, ume and ginger soba, squid ink soba, and more. A lineup that's exciting for anyone who wants to enjoy creative Okinawa soba, different from the standard sanmai-niku soba or soki soba.

That day, the blackboard in the shop said "¥200 cashback for customers ordering vegetable soba", so I ordered the limited-time recommendation, "vegetable soba", without hesitation.

Looking at the photo, it's piled high with leafy green stems, eggplant, green pepper, bell pepper, kombu, and more, and it looks quite hearty. On top of that there's chopped meat and bonito flakes too, so the bowl is lively 😊

When I first drank the soup, the flavor was quite different from the usual Okinawa soba pork bone or bonito broth. Maybe it's shiitake broth — a soup with a distinctive aroma and umami, light but with a strong character. If you're expecting the bonito aroma typical of Okinawa soba, you might be a little surprised. Perhaps the moisture from the vegetables was added too, but overall it was a gentle flavor, and I found it a bit lacking.

The vegetables are also quite different from a typical vegetable soba. The main ones were summer vegetables like leafy green stems, eggplant, green pepper, and bell pepper. The seasoning is very restrained — the type where you enjoy the vegetables' own aroma and texture. I think it's a bowl vegetable lovers will enjoy.

The noodles are said to be homemade hand-kneaded noodles — medium-thin and wavy. A bit on the thin side for Okinawa soba noodles.

The chopped meat on top was also something quite different from the usual Okinawa soba braised pork belly or pork spare ribs. Maybe a five-spice-like aroma — a distinctive seasoning that also felt a bit herbal, another flavor you rarely encounter at other shops. Both the soup and the toppings gave the impression of being finished as tropical-style creative cuisine rather than leaning toward the Okinawa soba standard. When I added a harissa-like condiment midway, a curry-like spiciness came out, and it changed into an even more ethnic-leaning bowl 🌶️

Honestly, in terms of my personal taste, the flavor didn't really click for me. Overall it was lightly seasoned, and quite different in direction from the powerful pork bone or bonito I look for in Okinawa soba. However, there really are a lot of vegetables, and by the time you finish you'll be quite full. I think it's an interesting shop for people who want to eat plenty of vegetables, people looking for an unusual variety different from the usual Okinawa soba, and people who like spicy or creative flavors.

Rating (9.5/15): Being inside Ginowan Yui Marche, it's easy to drop by while shopping, which is nice. If you want to feel the breadth of Okinawa soba, I'd really like you to try Papasubu's one-of-a-kind Okinawa soba at least once ✨

Noodles: ★★★✬☆（3.5）
Soup: ★★★☆☆（3）
Toppings: ★★★☆☆（3）

― 2nd visit (October 2024, Matsuo, Naha City) ―

I ate here in October 2024 😆 This time I visited "Okinawa Soba Papaya to Subui" in Matsuo, Naha City. I'd been once before when it was in Ishikawa, Uruma City, but this is my first visit since it moved to Naha City.

The shop is on the 1st floor of an old building, and when you go inside, a calm space with lots of houseplants spreads out, with an atmosphere just like a café 😊 Elementary school tables and chairs are used, which I felt helped create a cozy feel.

When I checked the menu on the meal ticket machine near the entrance, there was a range of creative Okinawa soba. Among them, the signature "Papasubu soba" caught my interest. I bought a ticket and waited at a table surrounded by plants. There were several other customers in the shop besides me, and everyone seemed to be spending a leisurely time.

While waiting in the shop, I looked out the window and it was raining. But somehow the atmosphere of this rainy day felt pleasant. The green of the houseplants, the old-fashioned furniture, and the natural light coming through the windows blend together in perfect balance 😌

After a while, the "Papasubu soba" was brought out in a yellow bowl.

First, as usual, I start with the soup. One sip, and a deep flavor unlike the Okinawa soba shops so far spreads out. In addition to pork, chicken, and bonito, the broth made from plenty of shiitake is its distinctive feature.

Actually, I felt this soup was delicious enough to work as a ramen soup rather than an Okinawa soba one. The strong shiitake flavor was especially memorable, a deep taste where you can also sense elements of Japanese dashi. This is a unique worldview you can hardly find at other shops 😋

The noodles are homemade hand-kneaded noodles with a gentle wave, and they have a moderate chewiness. They cling well to the soup, and eating them together seems to bring out the soup's umami even more. The thickness is about medium-thin, I'd say. The firmness is just right, and they kept a good texture right to the end.

The toppings, shibui and braised pork belly, are slowly simmered until melt-in-your-mouth tender. However, the braised pork belly still had a slightly sinewy quality, which bothered me a little. There's plenty of green onion on top, and eating it mixed with the soup adds a nice accent.

What was interesting was the condiment on the table. Instead of kōrēgūsu (chili-infused awamori), the usual Okinawa soba condiment, there was "Lemosco". It's a spicy condiment made with Setouchi lemons, and when I tried adding it to the soup, a nice sourness and heat were added, changing it into a completely new flavor! I was impressed by what a novel idea this is 😆

The way the atmosphere is created is distinctive too — the wooden tables like old school desks and the arrangement of houseplants seem to deliberately create a nostalgic space. A big houseplant is placed by the window, and beyond it you can see the kitchen. Every so often a lovely aroma of broth being made drifted from the kitchen, which whetted my appetite.

Rating (10.5/15): As a creative Okinawa soba, I felt it was a bowl with its own unique worldview. The distinctive soup with a strong shiitake flavor in particular is an appeal other shops don't have. It's wonderful that they've kept the same flavor even after moving to Naha City. Including the atmosphere of the shop, I think it's a valuable place where you can enjoy Okinawa soba at a relaxed pace.

Noodles: ★★★✬☆（3.5）
Soup: ★★★✬☆（3.5）
Toppings: ★★★✬☆（3.5）

― 1st visit (March 2023, Ishikawa, Uruma City) ―

*The following is an article from when it operated in Ishikawa, Uruma City. It has now moved to Ginowan City.

In March 2023 I went to "Papaya to Subui", located pretty much along Route 329.

This shop, which opened on April 11, 2022 in a renovated former ramen shop — I learned from TV or somewhere that they use papaya and subui, and shiitake for the soup broth, so I'd been curious about it.

"Papaya" seems to be a tropical fruit native to tropical America, but in Okinawa the green unripe fruit (green papaya) is eaten as a vegetable. In Okinawan cuisine it's often eaten as papaya champuru ☺ It's delicious.

"Subui" is winter melon (togan), a vegetable native to tropical Asia and a staple of Okinawan cuisine. This is delicious too.

From the meal ticket machine, I ordered the "Papasubu soba", whose name suggests these are used as toppings.

In a very stylish atmosphere inside the shop, first I took a sip of the soup 😀 Mmm, as expected it's different from the soups at other shops so far, and refreshing. Apparently, in addition to bonito, kombu, pork, and chicken broth, the soup is made from a large amount of shiitake and seasoned with Okinawan salt and sukugarasu. The homemade hand-kneaded noodles cling to the soup and are delicious 😋

The toppings are tender pork and, though it's hidden in the photo, melt-in-your-mouth subui (winter melon) soaked with flavor. There's also bonito flakes, papaya, julienned kombu, and shiitake, unique toppings you can't find at other shops, which is refreshing too. Healthy, isn't it.

Rating (10.5/15): The exterior and interior are very stylish, and the woman of the young tourist couple at the table next to me was really delighted 😀 Like, coming here was the right call lol. The hand-kneaded wavy noodles are delicious, and the toppings really are different from other shops, so it's refreshing. How about it, for those who are tired of regular Okinawa soba?

PS: However, I'm sorry to say, even though they went to the trouble of stylishly renovating the building and interior, the toilet in the separate building was old inside and not very clean, which I thought was a shame.
