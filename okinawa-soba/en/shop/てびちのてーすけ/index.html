Tebichi no Tesuke

てびちのてーすけ
Sashiki Shinkai, Nanjo CityRating 9/15

A pig's feet (tebichi) specialty shop recognizable by its laid-back pig sign. Its signature is the “Tesuke Special,” topped with pig's feet, braised pork belly, soft-bone pork ribs, yushi-dofu, and sea lettuce — everything. A light tebichi soba to enjoy with vinegar and shikuwasa pepper. They also sell seasoned frozen pig's feet and horse sashimi.

* This article was created with AI (Chat GPT 5 Thinking) and partly supplemented by me 😁

I ate here in December 2024 😆 This time I went to “Tebichi no Tesuke” in Sashiki Shinkai, Nanjo City.

In a corner of a well-worn building, a handwritten “Open” sign and a laid-back pig sign greet you boldly. As in the photo, “Tebichi specialty shop” is written in big letters, and artificial turf is laid in front of the shop, giving it a nice hideaway feel 🐷

Inside, the counter and tables are compactly arranged, with figurines casually displayed, giving it a relaxed, homey feel.

From the menu I ordered the “Tesuke Special” without hesitation. With the dignity of an “everything on top” bowl, pig's feet (tebichi), braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki), yushi-dofu, and sea lettuce (āsa) — the tastiest parts of Okinawa — are all packed into one bowl. The blended broth has a somewhat strong bonito stock and a noticeable saltiness.

The noodles are medium-thin fresh noodles, slightly wavy. They're chewy 🍜

The star, the pig's feet, is wobbly-soft. The skin melts, and sweetness spreads each time the layers of collagen unravel. The seasoning is quite restrained, and as the notice on the table says “we recommend adding vinegar,” a single drizzle brings out a sharp crispness all at once. The braised pork belly leans light. It has a little sinewy texture. The soft-bone pork ribs are soft right through to the bone.

The yushi-dofu topping is soft and gentle, and its sweetness swells further once it absorbs the broth. The green of the sea lettuce brings the scent of the sea, adding Okinawan flavor to the scene in the bowl, which is nice too. The condiments of shirogaki-negi (shredded green onion) and julienned ginger sharpen the outline, and it felt designed to run from start to finish as a “light tebichi soba.” There's no kōrēgūsu on the table; instead there is shikuwasa pepper, which pairs wonderfully. The citrus freshness lifts the broth's saltiness and the bonito aroma, and neatly pulls together the aftertaste. Toward the end, I dissolved a little chili paste, and the slow-building heat of the spice made my forehead sweat. Yet the outline of the broth doesn't collapse, and it let me drink it down happily to the end. By the time I finished eating, I was warmed from the core of my body 🔥

On the wall there is also a poster titled “How to eat tebichi deliciously,” and it's interesting that it carefully guides you through using vinegar, Tabasco, and even turning it into a broth-over-rice dish.

By the register there's also a notice for the “bone soup soba.” I thought I'd like to go for that next time. In the merchandise corner, in addition to seasoned frozen pig's feet, they even sell horse sashimi. It's an enticing lineup that makes you want to take it home as a snack for drinking at home, and my purse strings almost loosened. I felt that the reason for its popularity lies not only in the bowl but also in how it creates this kind of “room for enjoyment” 😊

Rating (9/15): All in all, it was a reassuring bowl for which the words “just plain tasty” fit perfectly, without any gimmicks. It pleasantly betrays the image that tebichi means rich and heavy, with a lightness that leaves no heaviness after the meal, which I like ✌️

When you come to Nanjo City, why not give it a try once?
