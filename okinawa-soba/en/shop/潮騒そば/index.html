Shiosai Soba

潮騒そば
Ishikawa, UrumaRating 9/15

An Okinawa soba shop on the first floor of "Hotel Shiosai," along the seaside in Ishikawa, Uruma, where you can also stay without meals. The signature "Shiosai Soba" (regular ¥1,080 / large ¥1,180) is a hearty bowl that even includes pig's feet, two big pieces of soft-bone pork ribs, offal, knotted kelp, and daikon radish. Bustling with local customers.

On this Okinawa soba journey, I visited "Shiosai Soba" in Ishikawa, Uruma City 🍜 I ate here in June 2025 😄

It's a shop on the first floor of "Hotel Shiosai," standing along the seaside in Ishikawa. The building has a "Hotel Shiosai" sign, and under it a "Shiosai Soba" banner stands, a rather rare arrangement where an Okinawa soba shop and a hotel are one 😆

Once inside, first a reception counter that looks like a hotel front desk greets you. Though I came to a soba shop, I get a curious feeling as if I'd stepped into a hotel lobby 😊

The seating area has a spacious layout, as if the hotel lobby had simply been turned into a diner. When I visited, it was quite bustling with local customers. It feels like a shop loved by the community, which is reassuring.

The menu has a notice: "All menu items available for takeout!!" Bringing your own container is also welcome, and you can tell locals use it casually.

Here's that soba menu. The signature "Shiosai Soba" comes with all of soft-bone pork ribs, offal (nakami), and tebichi, at ¥1,080 regular and ¥1,180 large. The others, offal soba, tebichi soba, soft-bone pork rib soba, and braised pork belly soba, are all ¥880 regular and ¥980 large. And for +¥150 you can apparently switch to fresh noodles (limited quantity) 🤤

Beef tendon and bean sprout soba, meatless plain soba, and yushi-dofu soba are available too.

What also caught my eye was the limited-quantity "bone soup." With a small side dish and rice it's ¥700, and the bone soup soba is a great-value ¥780 regular. It ends when it runs out, so I'd like to aim for it next time.

Set meals, rice bowls, a la carte items, and drinks are all available, so its range as a diner is wide too.

Now, this time I asked for the "Shiosai Soba," named after the shop, in the large size. This is what was brought out. A bowl with a big piece of tebichi, two large soft-bone pork ribs, offal, knotted kelp, and, unusually, even daikon radish, a bowl with seriously hearty volume ✨

First, the soup. Maybe it's a blended broth; it feels like the pork bone comes through firmly, but the liquid from the offal soup also seems to be mixed in, so honestly it was a complex flavor I couldn't pin down with a single word 😋

The noodles are thin, loosely wavy noodles, boiled on the soft side.

The toppings are really something, though. The tebichi is large, there are two big soft-bone pork ribs, plus offal and knotted kelp. Meeting daikon radish in Okinawa soba is quite rare, and it was a welcome little discovery 😄 Partly because I got the large, I was completely full by the time I finished.

And as I mentioned at the start, this is a hotel, so you can also stay without meals. A single room is ¥4,500 per night without meals, and a twin room is ¥8,000 (for 2 guests), quite reasonable. Thinking about it, being able to eat Okinawa soba and then stay right there may be a rather luxurious way to use it 🍶

Rating (9/15): A somewhat elusive soup, a blended broth with a strong pork-bone flavor mixed with the flavor of the offal soup. The noodles are thin, loosely wavy, and soft. With tebichi, two soft-bone pork ribs, offal, knotted kelp, and daikon radish, the volume of toppings is beyond complaint. My impression of the flavor itself was average, but its satisfying portions made it understandable why it's bustling with local customers 😊
