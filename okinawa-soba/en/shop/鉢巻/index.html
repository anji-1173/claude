Hachimaki

鉢巻
Chuo, Okinawa City (Koza)Rating 9/15

A unique shop in the Koza streetscape with two faces: a soba shop by day and "Bar Samurai" by night (* it may have closed as of 2026). Look for the red gate and the words NEW YORK RESTAURANT. A gentle, light pork-bone soup and rare thin, wavy noodles.

* As of July 2026 it may have closed, so please be aware.

On this Okinawa soba journey, I'll introduce "Hachimaki" in Chuo, Okinawa City. I went to eat here in May 2025 😄

I visited "Hachimaki," in the streetscape of Koza in Chuo, Okinawa City. The exterior is quite unique: a gate like a red torii with the words "NEW YORK RESTAURANT," and red paper lanterns and decorations reading "Sakaba (tavern)" hanging from it, giving off a rather mysterious atmosphere 😊

No wonder, since this shop actually also operates as a bar by night, under the name "Samurai (Bar Samurai)." Looking at the banners, as the soba shop "Hachimaki" it's open 11:00 am to 3:00 pm and 5:00 pm to 10:00 pm, and as the night bar "Samurai" from 10:00 pm to 5:00 am, truly a shop with two faces, day and night 🍶

Inside, it's built just like a bar, with corrugated metal walls, a tapestry of a samurai under the moon, and red, pink, and green paper lanterns hanging, creating a unique world. There's a sofa seating area too, so it feels less like a daytime Okinawa soba shop and more like visiting a nighttime establishment 😅 There was even a sign saying "Weekdays only: karaoke, all-you-can-sing ¥500," which clearly conveys that nights are the main business.

Honestly, my impression is that Okinawa soba shops that are open at night often have strongly seasoned flavors, probably with a finishing bowl in mind. So I thought this one would be that kind too, but in a good way my expectations were betrayed.

Now, let's look at the menu. Okinawa soba (medium) ¥850, (large) ¥950, tebichi (pig's feet) soba ¥1,000, sets with jushi (Okinawan seasoned rice), and even jushi taco rice. This time I went with the classic and ordered the "Okinawa soba (large)" 🍜

And what's interesting is that you can choose the noodles from "wavy noodles" and "flat noodles." Since I was there, I asked the staff member for a recommendation, and they pushed the wavy noodles, so I obediently chose the wavy noodles.

After a while, the Okinawa soba (large) appeared! Topped with braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), egg, fish cake (kamaboko), and green onions, it's a very standard-looking bowl 😄

First, the soup. Mm, this is pork-bone based, but only a faint pork-bone flavor, a very gentle taste. Since it's a shop that's also open at night, I had braced myself for a strong flavor, so this was a welcome surprise. It's light and goes down smoothly.

Next, the noodles. The wavy noodles I chose are actually quite rare. I don't remember seeing thin, wavy noodles much in Okinawa soba. And they're finished soft. Since the soup is gentle, these thin wavy noodles seem to cling to it nicely.

Looking at the braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) topping, the flavor has soaked in moderately, which is nice. However, some parts still had a bit of sinewy texture. The egg and fish cake are the usual reliable supporting players.

Overall, in contrast to its unique appearance as a nighttime bar, "Hachimaki"'s Okinawa soba was a gentle, light, and, in a good way, ordinary bowl. The novelty of thin, wavy noodles was a small discovery 😊

Rating (9/15): While sightseeing in Koza, why not stop by "Hachimaki (Samurai)" and enjoy its two faces: Okinawa soba by day and a bar by night? 👋
