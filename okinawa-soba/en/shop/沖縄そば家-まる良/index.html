Okinawa Soba-ya Maruyoshi

沖縄そば家 まる良
Kanna, Ginoza VillageClosedRating 9/15

A shop in Kanna, Ginoza Village, that opened in summer 2024 (*closed in May 2025). Its distinctive feature was choosing between Chinen Seimenjo's wavy noodles and Sankakuya Seimenjo's Nago soba noodles, and its crunchy soft-bone pork ribs were unique too. I'm keeping the article as a record.

*This shop appears to have closed on May 7, 2025. What a shame.

This time I'm introducing "Okinawa Soba-ya Maruyoshi" in Kanna, Ginoza Village. I went to eat here in March 2025😄

Ginoza Village is an area a bit north of central Naha, and I stopped by as part of a drive up north. When you enter the parking lot, several banner flags are standing, and you can see at a glance that there's plenty of space. The building is relatively new, with a clean white exterior; above the glass door hangs a banner reading "Okinawa Soba-ya Maruyoshi," and there was an "Open" notice at the entrance too😄

Inside, the interior is bright and simple, with table seats lined up. Panels explaining the types of noodles are posted on the wall, and this shop's big feature is that "you can choose your noodles"🎉

Opening the menu, it's set up so you can choose from two kinds of noodles: one is Chinen Seimenjo's "Chinen soba (wavy noodles)" and the other is Sankakuya Seimenjo's "Nago soba (thin, straight noodles)." Chinen soba is satisfying, characterized by chewy springiness and wavy noodles that snap off with a pop, while Nago soba is a firm straight noodle widely used in northern Okinawa, with reliable deliciousness. I was curious about both, but this time I followed the staff's recommendation and chose Chinen Seimenjo's "wavy noodles."

Besides an orthodox lineup of soki soba, braised pork belly soba, pig's feet (tebichi) soba, soft-bone pork rib soba, and so on, I was also curious about an unusual one, the "vegetable soba" limited to 5 servings a day😁 The vegetable soba is a Maruyoshi-style dish topped with garlic-flavored stir-fried garlic chives, carrots, bean sprouts, cabbage, and pork, so I'd like to try it next time. This time I ordered the "Maruyoshi soba (medium)." You can also choose the noodle amount from small 150g, medium 200g, or large 250g, which is nice for people who want to eat heartily.

Looking at the Maruyoshi soba that was brought out after a while, it was a visibly hearty bowl, packed with toppings starting with the soft-bone pork ribs😋

First, the customary gulp of broth. It's bonito-broth-based, and I got the impression it's finished quite rich compared to other shops😊 But rather than a standout personality, my impression was of plainly tasty bonito broth.

Next, I try the wavy noodles I chose. Eating them, they were finished on the soft side overall. They had the popping texture typical of wavy noodles, but personally I felt they could have had a bit more springiness🍜

When I picked up the soft-bone pork ribs topping with my chopsticks, the cartilage part had a really crunchy texture, a type you rarely get at other shops😲 While many shops simmer their soft-bone ribs until meltingly tender, Maruyoshi's cartilage keeps a firm texture, and I thought it's rare for it to be this crunchy. It may divide opinions. On the other hand, the meat part has a bit of sinewy texture.

Rating (9/15): Overall, honestly, I'd say it's average~. It's a soba shop rooted in the community here in the Kanna area of Ginoza Village✨ It also has a bit of personality, with the fun of choosing your noodles and the crunchy soft-bone pork ribs, so why not stop by when you're on a drive up north?😊
