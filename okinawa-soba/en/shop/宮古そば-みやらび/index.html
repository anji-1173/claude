Miyako Soba Miyarabi

宮古そば みやらび
Gushi, Naha (near the airport)Rating 9/15

A Miyako soba specialty shop with a reddish-brown triangular roof, 10 minutes by car from Naha Airport. A bowl with a gentle pork-bone-based soup, thin straight noodles with a round cross-section, and braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) and lean pork on top. The interior has a Showa-retro feel, and turnover is quick.

I went to eat here in December 2024 😄

About 10 minutes by car from Naha Airport, a reddish-brown triangular roof appears all on its own in a corner of Gushi. That's “Miyako Soba Miyarabi” 🚗💨. A well-worn “Open” sign hangs from the wooden pillar at the entrance, and the purple flag proudly shows the words “Miyako Soba” in white. It makes your heart race.

　Right when you open the door, there's the meal ticket machine. More than 50 buttons, big and small, are lined up, and handwritten notes like “10-yen coins cannot be used” and “Lunch service” announcements are pasted all over it. Basic soba starts from around ¥700, and I'm surprised by the breadth of the lineup, which includes sets, soups, and even champuru (Okinawan stir-fry) 😲. This time I pushed the button without hesitation for the signature menu item that's even in the shop's name, “Miyako soba (¥750).” As soon as I put the meal ticket on the counter, cooking began briskly in the kitchen in the back.

　The wall side of the L-shaped counter inside has flower-patterned, mosaic-style glass, a bit like a Showa-retro coffee shop. At lunchtime it's sure to be full, but turnover is quick, and from handing over the meal ticket until the bowl arrives felt like about 5 minutes. A welcome speed for someone who wants to drop in quickly between sightseeing ⏱️.

　Now, the Miyako soba that appeared in front of me. The soup, clear yet slightly cloudy, has the gentle aroma of a pork bone base. It's not the “original Miyako style” where the toppings hide under the noodles; instead, the braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) and lean pork (loin) sit proudly on top. Pale green chopped green onions float on it, and although it looks simple, it's a bowl with a certain dignity.

　First, a sip with the soup spoon. It's pork bone based.

　The noodles are thin and straight with a round cross-section, typical of Miyako soba. The firmness is modest, on the soft side 🍜.

　The braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) has a just-right balance of fat and lean, while the lean pork has a solid bite. Both have a sinewy texture 😆.

　When I suddenly looked around, the place was bustling with customers. Even though it's located near the airport, it doesn't go all-in on tourist-spot prices; it's a shop that stays close to local daily life, and that atmosphere fills the shop, making it comfortable.

Rating (9/15): There's no standout flashiness, but it was a Miyako soba that embodies “just plain delicious” and stays close to everyday life. That's what I felt Miyarabi's Miyako soba is. It's a little away from central Naha City, but it's perfect for filling up before returning your rental car or for lunch on the way to the airport. How about trying it at least once ✨. 🥢
