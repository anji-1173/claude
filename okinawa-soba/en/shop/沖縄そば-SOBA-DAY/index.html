Okinawa Soba SOBA DAY

沖縄そば SOBA DAY
Daido, NahaRating 9/15

A counter-only shop run by an owner who trained at my recommended shop "EIBUN." The shop name comes from "Okinawa Soba Day" on October 17. The signature is the double-meat soba, with chashu, braised pork belly, and fu (wheat gluten) on a broth with plenty of bonito.

This article was created with AI (ChatGPT-4o), with some additions of my own😁

This time I visited "Okinawa Soba SOBA DAY" in Daido, Naha. I ate here in October 2024😁 The shop's exterior is simple, and the mostly white sign catches the eye✨.

When you enter the shop, the ticket machine jumps out at you🎟. It seems to be a meal ticket system here, and the menu is extensive. What I chose this time was the "double-meat soba"! A luxurious bowl where you can enjoy both braised pork belly and chashu. When I bought my ticket and took a seat, the counter seats were full and it was bustling. It really is a popular shop😊.

This shop's name is "Okinawa Soba SOBA DAY," and indeed "Okinawa Soba Day" is October 17😄

Since I came to eat around Okinawa Soba Day, there was also a campaign pork dish called "Pineapple Pork." This time I went with the regular "double-meat soba," but it seems the usual broth had been upgraded to one made with "Pineapple Pork," so I was looking forward to it😆

The "double-meat soba" was brought out after a wait of a few minutes. It's a very beautiful-looking bowl, with colorful toppings. Chashu, braised pork belly, and... fu (wheat gluten)? were on top. The fu was a little unexpected, with a distinctive fluffy texture. It has almost no taste of its own.

They said the broth uses Pineapple Pork, but the bonito broth really comes through in it🍜.

The noodles are medium-thin and straight, with a slightly firm texture. The overall balance is good, but if you ask whether there's any distinctive feature worth noting, it feels ordinary.

And as for the meat, both the braised pork belly and chashu are the type where a bit of sinewy texture remains. This may divide likes and dislikes😅. They're simmered thoroughly, but in terms of tenderness I'd say they're one step short.

The shop's atmosphere is casual, and it seemed bustling with local customers. It's a compact setup with only counter seats, and it's a nice point that it's easy to visit casually even on your own. Also, island chili peppers and vinegar are provided as condiments, so you can customize it to your liking, which is nice🌶️.

By the way, when I googled "SOBA DAY," it seems to be run by someone who previously trained at "EIBUN," which I introduce in "Recommended Okinawa soba shops I'd like you to try at least once." That raised my expectations, but my impression of the actual taste was just plain tasty. Of course, it's a reliable bowl.

Rating (9/15): Overall, it was plainly tasty Okinawa soba. I wanted one more surprise. It might be good to stop by once when visiting as a tourist, but whether you'd come back may depend on your taste? Next time I'd like to try a different dish🍴!
