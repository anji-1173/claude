A diner in Chibana, Okinawa City, run by an owner who loves pottery (*closed November 22, 2024). A gentle soup with bonito broth and fall-apart soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki). Shisa made by the owner lined the shop. I'm keeping this article as a record.

I ate here in November 2024 😄🍜　I went to "Wakigawaya" in Chibana, Okinawa City! The exterior has a bit of a "Kitanashuran" (shabby-but-delicious) vibe, but it's exactly these kinds of shops where you can expect good food!

When you enter, the dishes made by the owner, a pottery enthusiast, catch your eye. Handwritten menus line the walls, written not only in Japanese but also in English. Perhaps because of the location, there were foreign customers too, and it left an impression how lively it was, with them mixed in among the locals.

On top of that, the savory smell of champuru (Okinawan stir-fry) drifted through the shop, whetting my appetite 🤤.

This time I ordered the "Soki Soba." The bowl brought before me was filled with a gentle soup rich with bonito broth. The soup is light, but you can clearly taste the bonito flavor, and it's a soothing taste with every sip.

The noodles are medium-thick wavy noodles, finished on the soft side. The noodle lengths seem uneven. The soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki) are lightly seasoned and stewed until tender. They're so soft they fall apart when you put them in your mouth, but a little sinewy texture remains in the cartilage part.

At this shop, a notice caught my eye: "We will close as of November 22, 2024."

It's sad that a shop loved by locals for many years is closing, but I'm glad I got to eat here at this timing.

Looking around the shop after the meal, there was a row of ceramic shisa pieces apparently made by the owner, and it was a space with a warmth that felt very Okinawan.

Rating (9/15): Overall my impression was "solidly tasty Okinawa soba," but with the shop's atmosphere and local feel added in, it was a heartwarming bowl 😊. It was right before closing, but I'm glad I got to eat here.
