Ganeko Soba

我如古そば
Gusukuma, Urasoe CityRating 9/15

A traditional diner in Gusukuma, Urasoe City. Its signature is the Ganeko soba, with a gentle, translucent pork-bone broth and thick, wavy noodles. There is also a free fried chicken service on Saturdays and a free jushi service on the 14th of every month. A popular local spot with a full lineup of set meals.

* This article was created by AI (Claude Opus 4.5) with some additions and updates by me 😆 I ate here in February 2025 😄

This time I visited “Ganeko Soba” in Gusukuma, Urasoe City! Looking at the exterior, the words “Ganeko Soba” on the wooden sign are striking. A pink noren hangs at the entrance, giving off the feel of an old-fashioned Okinawa soba shop 🏠

Inside, the two-tone orange and blue walls stand out, and it has a nostalgic diner style. A few table seats are lined up, and handwritten menu items like “tebichi nitsuke (braised pig’s feet),” “nakami soba,” and “tebichi soba” are posted on the walls ✨

Looking at the menu, it’s not just Okinawa soba but a full lineup of set meals too. There is “braised pig’s feet set meal” ¥1,200, “soki soup” ¥1,200, “nakami soup” ¥890, “liver and vegetable set” ¥930, “ginger pork set” ¥950, “vegetable champuru” ¥850, “katsudon” ¥880, “champon” ¥880, and more — an abundant lineup typical of an Okinawan diner 🍽️

The Okinawa soba menu also offers plenty of variety: “soba set meal” ¥930, “Okinawa soba” ¥700, “sanmai-niku soba” ¥750, “soki soba” ¥800, “tebichi soba” ¥980, “nakami soba” ¥890, “mugwort soba” ¥800, “squid-ink soba” ¥800, and so on. Kids’ soba at ¥400 is also a nice point for families 👨‍👩‍👧

And I found a bargain campaign too! Every Saturday is “Fried Chicken Day,” with 2 pieces of fried chicken free with a soba order 🎉 Also, the 14th of every month is “Jushi Day,” with one bowl of jushi free with a soba order. You can apparently swap it for white rice if you like. Today happened to be the 14th, so I got the jushi as a free service! Lucky 😆

This time I ordered the “Ganeko Soba” (¥820), which shares its name with the shop. After a short wait, it was brought out together with the jushi 🍜

In the bowl is a translucent pork-bone broth with thick, slightly wavy noodles. The toppings are two slices of braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), fish cake (kamaboko), and green onions. It looks satisfying just by appearance 😋

First, a sip of the broth. It has the gentle flavor of pork-bone stock. It has no off-notes and is a comforting taste. The saltiness is just right, and it’s an easy-drinking broth.

Next, the noodles. They are thick, wavy noodles with a soft texture. A simple flavor typical of Okinawa soba 🍜

The braised pork belly and bone-in pork ribs toppings are both lightly seasoned. The braised pork belly has a decent balance of fat and lean, but has a bit of a sinewy texture. The bone-in pork ribs are the same: the seasoning is gentle, but there were parts where the sinew remained.

I also had the complimentary jushi. This too is lightly seasoned, like a simple Okinawan seasoned rice. Eating it together with the soba fills you right up 🍚

Looking around the shop, many local customers were coming in and it was lively. Many were ordering set meals, and I felt this is a shop beloved by the locals. It’s very ordinary Okinawa soba, but that “ordinary” is exactly what’s good about it 😊

Rating (9/15): It was a very ordinary, tasty Okinawa soba. It has no special distinguishing character, but it’s a reassuring bowl that makes you understand why the locals love it. With such a full set-meal lineup, you’ll want to try things other than Okinawa soba too. If you come to Urasoe City, why not drop by? 😊
