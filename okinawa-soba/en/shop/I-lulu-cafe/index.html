I lulu cafe

Isa, Ginowan CityUniqueRating 9/15

A Thai food cafe along National Route 58. Its specialty is the "Thai-style Okinawa soba", with a pho-like chicken broth topped with shibui (winter melon), chicken thigh and cilantro. You can also change up the flavor with lemon juice, fish sauce and chili. A stylish shop that also sells clothes in the next room.

I went to eat here in November 2023 😊　It was during the "13th Okinawa Soba Stamp Rally (2023.10.1–2024.2.29)", so I wanted to eat at a participating shop, but unluckily both of the two shops I went to were temporarily closed, so although it's not a stamp rally shop, I decided to eat at this shop that I'd been curious about for a while.

I'd seen this shop every time I drove along Route 58, but what concerned me was the parking. I'd thought you could park a few cars in front of the shop entrance, but looking carefully this time, it seemed difficult.

So while I was thinking "What should I do?", I noticed something that looked like a parking lot a little way off.

There were signs for several shops on the sign at the entrance, and looking carefully, on a small ocher-based sign was the name "I' LULUCAFE" too 🤗

However, the time I went this time was right at lunchtime, and with space for about 5 cars, and it being a parking lot shared by several shops, it was completely full. (The ramen shop next door seems to be popular too.)

But after waiting a little, a space opened up, and I somehow managed to park.

I entered the shop and ordered the "Thai-style Okinawa soba". While waiting, I checked out the menu 😊

After looking through the menu and glancing around the shop, I saw an entrance leading to the next room.

When I went in, it seemed they also sell clothes, perhaps an original brand. It had a stylish feel 🤗

After a while, the "Thai-style Okinawa soba" came 😁

There were various condiments next to the soba. The staff explained that they're lemon juice, sugar, chili and fish sauce, and to add them to taste. Thai cooking condiments, this is a new kind of Okinawa soba not found at other shops lol

First I wanted to taste the broth as is, so I took a sip without adding anything.

"?" It's completely different from the Okinawa soba broths I've had so far. A flavor I'd never tasted before.

I learned about this shop when I saw it featured in the newspaper, and when I checked the newspaper article I'd snapped a photo of, it said that rather than the standard bonito and pork stock used in the usual Okinawa soba broth, it seems to be the broth used in a dish called pho, a chicken stock. A new broth, different from the Okinawa soba I've had until now.

Next I had the noodles. They're thick, straight, on-the-soft-side noodles. The topping is shibui (winter melon), soft and well soaked in flavor. Since shibui was also a topping at "Okinawa Soba Papaya to Subui" in Uruma City, which I introduced before, it reminded me of that.

The meat is chicken thigh rather than pork, and tender. There was cilantro in it too, and it was probably my first time eating it (;^ω^); I think it's an ingredient people either love or hate, but I didn't notice any particularly strong taste or aroma and ate it just fine lol　I have a dumb tongue lol

Partway through, I added the Thai condiments (lemon juice, fish sauce, chili) and enjoyed changing up the flavor. Maybe I added too little lemon juice and fish sauce, since I didn't feel much change. But the chili, maybe I put in too much, because from partway through I was eating with sweat pouring out (;^ω^)　Spicy.

Rating (9/15): A new kind of Okinawa soba. The Okinawa soba I know has turned into Thai food 😊　How about everyone trying this Okinawa soba at least once?
