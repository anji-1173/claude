Uehara Soba

上原そば
Yohena, NagoProvisional #5RecommendedRating 13.5/15

Provisional #5 (13.5/15). An "Okinawa soba and local fish cuisine" shop on high ground with a sweeping view of the sea. Cloudy, MSG-free soup with Agu broth × homemade noodles with whole wheat flour and bonito powder. The bone-in pork ribs on a separate plate and the small dish of local fish sashimi are nice touches.

✨I ate here in February 2025. This time it started with a small mishap 😅 I headed excitedly for "Suba Suba" on Yagaji Island, but it was unexpectedly their regular day off... It had been featured on the local Okinawan TV program "Uchiran", so my expectations were high, and honestly the shock was big. But I switched gears, telling myself that Okinawa soba touring is fun including twists like this, and decided to look for a shop open nearby. That's where I came across "Okinawa Soba and Local Fish Cuisine Uehara Soba" in Yohena, Nago City.

The building's exterior looks like an apartment, so for a moment I felt a little uneasy, wondering if this was really the right place, but a "UEHARA SOBA" sign welcomes you clearly. The sign at the entrance lists the business hours, and there's a homey atmosphere.

It's a meal ticket machine style with an extensive menu. Besides the standards like hon-soki soba, tebichi soba, and sanmai-niku soba, there's also a sea grape rice bowl on the menu.

When you go inside, the first thing that jumps out at you is the view from the windows. It's on high ground, so the location with a sweeping view of the sea is the best 🌊 It was a bit cloudy that day, but even so the view was plenty refreshing. With wooden tables and calm lighting, it's a stylish, café-like space. It gives the impression of a dining room with a sea view rather than an Okinawa soba shop.

This time I ordered the "hon-soki soba". You can choose between rich and light soup, and when I asked the staff, they said "The rich one is ordered a lot". I obediently followed the recommendation and chose rich 😄 The bowl that was brought out has a striking cloudy white soup. Apparently they use Agu broth, and a soft, gentle aroma rises with the steam.

First, a sip of the soup. Smooth and creamy, a perfect balance 😋 It's a different type from Kame Soba's Agu broth, and the flavor reminded me of Getto in Naha City and Tsukikurogi in Uruma City. Delicious 😋

The noodles are homemade, medium-thin and straight, with whole wheat flour and bonito powder kneaded in. These are great too. They have solid chewiness. They go well with the cloudy soup, which clings to them nicely. Personally, these were noodles very much to my liking.

The bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), served on a separate plate, look hearty right from the start. When you pick them up with chopsticks there's a bit of a sinewy texture, but in return you can enjoy a properly meaty texture. The seasoning is a little on the sweet side, well soaked in, and simply tasty 😋 Dipping them in the soup gives them yet another character. What made me even happier was the small dish of sashimi. A style you rarely see at other shops, and it shows how much effort they put into local fish cuisine. Sashimi being served at a soba shop is pretty rare, isn't it.

Rating (13.5/15): It was a pinch-hitter shop I went into on short notice, but in the end it was a big hit. Location, soup, noodles, toppings — whichever you look at, the level is high, and honestly I can proudly say it's "recommended-shop level 😄". It's a shop I'd love both tourists and locals to visit at least once. High level. If you like Agu-broth-style Okinawa soba, please give it a try.
