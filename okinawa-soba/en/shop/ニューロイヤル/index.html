Okinawa Soba Journey

New Royal

ニューロイヤル
Kinaka, Uruma CityRating 9/15

A stylish Okinawa soba shop in Kinaka, Uruma City, opened in December 2023. It used to be a mozuku seaweed shop, and comes with a “How to Eat Course” that makes it fun to change the flavor with original condiments such as umai-ume (plum) and shikuwasa vinegar. The signature is the Royal Soba, which also has yushi-dofu from the local Shirin-gawa Tofu.

I went to eat here in January 2024 😊 It was during the “13th Okinawa Soba Stamp Rally (2023.10.1–2024.2.29)” and I was one shop away from clearing it. I wanted to eat at a participating shop, but there was no final-shop candidate in Uruma City, where I had business, so I ate at this shop that I happened to find online 😄

When I googled this shop later, I found it had just opened in December 2023 (^0^;) As usual, no research (^0^;)

Perhaps because it had just opened, even on a weekday lunchtime it was bustling with local customers, and quite a few were in school uniforms (junior high? high school?) 😄

And I was surprised when I went inside! With apologies, the location is rather countryside-like by Okinawa standards, and the quite stylish interior seemed out of place in the area? (🙏 sorry)

I thought it was a stylish interior just like “OKINAWA SOBA HITOTSU” in Wakasa, Naha 😆

Inside there is a ticket machine, and a staff member guided me to buy a ticket. Since it’s a new shop, I’d like them to also introduce cashless payment; unfortunately it seems to be cash only (^0^;)

I ordered the “Royal Soba,” which shares the shop’s name, and waited at the counter seat the staff showed me to 😄

When I sat at the counter, there was an explanation called the “How to Eat Course.” The note “Please post it on social media freely” is something new that other shops don’t have, lol. The owner seems to be skilled at marketing that makes full use of social media 😁

If customers who come to eat promote the shop themselves, it will spread through word of mouth as free advertising — impressive, a strategy that matches the present day.

I also try my best to share information on my soba blog and X, so I would have loved to photograph the stylish interior in all sorts of ways, but it was crowded, and there were many young students too, and I’d be embarrassed if an old guy snapping away with a smartphone put people off and I worried about it, so I held back and couldn’t photograph the inside of the shop. Sorry 🙏😆

The “Royal Soba” has arrived 😁 I also learned this later by googling, but a small bowl of mozuku is served first, and I enjoyed it; this shop apparently used to be a mozuku shop and then opened an Okinawa soba shop.

Okinawa soba × mozuku reminds me of “Chodegwa” in Nishihara Town 😆

Recalling that, first I took my usual sip of the broth 😄

It’s a blended broth of bonito and pork bone, with the pork-bone stock coming through, a light and gentle type of pork-bone stock. I thought the flavor of the broth was about the same as shops with an ordinary pork-bone stock.

The noodles aren’t visible in the photo, but they’re medium-thick and apparently use fresh noodles; they were slurpy, but not very firm and relatively soft.

As for the toppings, the braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) is sliced quite thin, which is different from other shops. The pork spare ribs (soki) are tender, but a little sinewy and there were dry spots too.

At the shops I’d eaten at so far, the yushi-dofu used “Unashi Tofu” of Itoman City, but this time, the shop being in Uruma City, they said they use yushi-dofu from the local Uruma City “Shirin-gawa Tofu.”

But sorry 🙏 I have a dull tongue and couldn’t really tell the difference between “Unashi Tofu” and Shirin-gawa Tofu. The Shirin-gawa Tofu yushi-dofu I had this time seemed a little firmer?

After enjoying the Royal Soba as served, I enjoyed changing the flavor as described in the “How to Eat Course” 😄

On the table were these condiments: ichimi chili, pickled red ginger, yuzu pepper, umai-ume, shikuwasa vinegar, and kōrēgūsu (chili-infused awamori).

First I added a little “umai-ume” and tasted the noodles and broth. I tried it wondering “does plum paste go with pork-bone stock and Okinawa soba?” but I was surprised (°°)

It was an ordinary pork-bone-stock broth, but it was changed into a refreshing broth with a hint of sourness and noodles with a new taste 😁 It goes well, delicious.

Next I added “shikuwasa vinegar” to the broth. This too changed the ordinary pork-bone-stock broth into a light broth with a hint of sourness. It reminded me of the lemongrass broth at “Kissa Jan” in Yaese Town that I ate a few days ago. However, Jan is bonito-based and this is pork-bone-based, so the flavors differ.

Rating (9/15): It’s just plain tasty. As it’s a newly opened shop, I really hope they go cashless. The interior is stylish and welcoming to social media, so I recommend it to anyone who wants to post photogenic content. The flavor-change approach, using original condiments on a pork-bone-forward broth, feels new. The braised pork belly is thinly sliced, and the soki, though tender, is a little dry and sinewy.
