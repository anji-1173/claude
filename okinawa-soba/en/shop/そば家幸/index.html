Sobaya Sachi

そば家幸
Kanna, Ginoza VillageRating 9/15

In Kanna, Ginoza Village, marked by a warehouse-like exterior along National Route 329. A rare shop open only on Saturdays and Sundays, its interior is packed with pro baseball goods such as the Yomiuri Giants. The signature dish is the Sachi Soba with a blended broth where the richness of pork bone comes through. They also ship soba.

*This article was created with "ChatGPT 4o" with some additions by me. I went to eat here in January 2025 😄

This time again, in search of Okinawa soba, I drove north and further north, and where I ended up was "Sobaya Sachi" in Kanna, Ginoza Village. It has a rugged, warehouse-like exterior 😄

The moment you step inside, uniforms and goods from pro baseball's Yomiuri Giants and others are displayed all over the walls, with huge impact ⚾️✨

This shop is a rare one, open "only on Saturdays and Sundays". This time I visited at lunchtime on a Saturday, and cars kept pulling into the parking lot one after another; it was full of local customers and people who seemed to be tourists. Inside there are several table seats, with a relaxed, calm feel. There are also lots of players' autographs and photos on the walls, making it an irresistible space for baseball fans 📸

Now, what I ordered this time was the "Sachi Soba", which shares the shop's name. There were several kinds on the menu, but first I'm going with the signature dish~ 🍜

The bowl that was brought out has a very Okinawan yachimun-style design, and just the look of it gets me excited 🎵　In addition to braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) and bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), there's fish cake (kamaboko) on top too, so it has quite a lot of volume. First, a sip of the broth… yep, a blended stock with the richness of pork bone gently coming through, and the saltiness is a bit strong. My impression is that rather than the light type, it's the solid type of broth.

The noodles are medium-thick straight noodles, glossy in appearance ✨　Boiled on the soft side.

And then the meats… first, the bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki). These are surprisingly lightly seasoned, and rather than melt-in-your-mouth, they have a firm texture where the fibers remain. Quite a lot of sinew. The braised pork belly (sanmai-niku) also has a somewhat strong sinewy texture.

Rating (9/15): All in all, it feels plain tasty 👍　Even if you're not a baseball fan you can enjoy it, but it's a shop I'd really like you to visit once when you come to the northern region during camp season. You might be a little overwhelmed by the number of uniforms and photos on display (lol)
