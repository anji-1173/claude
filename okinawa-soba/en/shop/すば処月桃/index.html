Suba-dokoro Getto

すば処月桃
Yorimiya, NahaRating 9.5/15

A shop with an additive-free stock of ma-kombu kelp, bonito and pork bone, and homemade wood-ash noodles. The smooth, rich pork bone broth is high-level. The specialty is the Shishimai Suba topped with braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki) and bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki).

I ate here in November 2023. This shop was a participant in the "13th Okinawa Soba Stamp Rally (2023.10.1–2024.2.29)", and I went as my 23rd of 34 shops 😊

This shop wasn't a standalone house; it seemed to be in the 1st floor of a building-like structure. Judging from the address, maybe it's a JA building?

It was lunchtime on a holiday, but when I opened the door and went in, it was completely full (*_*)　I thought this must be a shop really loved by local customers.

But since it's Okinawa soba, I figured turnover would be fast and decided to wait a little. Then several groups left, so I made it inside safely 🤗

For my order, I got the "Shishimai Soba (rich)" introduced in the stamp rally booklet. When I said to the staff, "Nikumai soba, rich please," pointing at the booklet, they replied, "Shishimai soba, rich, right."　I had the reading wrong. In Okinawan dialect, meat is "shishi" (;^ω^)　My apologies lol

While waiting I checked the menu. Apparently these are homemade noodles made using the supernatant of wood ash (lye).

Wood-ash noodles may be a first for me since I had "hijaa (goat) soba" at "Mukashi Mukashi" in Nago.

However, even after a while since ordering, it just wouldn't come; at other shops, even when full, it comes in a reasonable time, but at this shop it just didn't come (;^ω^)

It seems the operation is being run by 2 elderly women, which is tough (;^ω^)

It finally came. First, the broth of course. According to the explanation, the broth: "We carefully make the stock with Hokkaido ma-kombu kelp, bonito and pork bone. The additive-free stock has a deeply nourishing flavor where you can feel savoriness that spreads naturally in your mouth and a powerful richness." 😁

Taking a sip, the rich pork bone stock comes through. It's smooth; I think it's richer and more delicious than the agu-stock Kame Soba or the rich pork bone stock of "Tsukikuruchi" I ate the day before. However, the saltiness is also on the strong side.

I think the broth is smooth, rich and delicious, but as expected it can't beat "796 Nankuru", which I introduce as a recommended shop 😊

Next I have the noodles. They're a medium-thin straight type. According to the explanation, "The homemade wood-ash noodles have a good aroma, and you can feel a springy chewiness, smooth texture going down, and a faint sweetness."

However, when I ate them, I couldn't feel the aroma or flavor of wood ash, and I think the ones I ate before at "Mukashi Mukashi" in Nago City had more flavor.

However, the noodles themselves are smooth and I think plain tasty. These noodles also come across a bit salty.

The toppings have volume with braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki) and bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), but the hon-soki had a bit of sinewy texture and felt dry.

Rating (9.5/15): Plain tasty. The pork bone broth is fairly smooth and delicious, but a bit on the salty side. The noodles are smooth and plain tasty too, but these also come across salty. The operation when it's full is a bit disappointing.
