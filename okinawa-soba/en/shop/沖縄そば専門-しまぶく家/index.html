Okinawa Soba Senmon Shimabuku-ya

沖縄そば専門 しまぶく家

I went to eat here in February 2025 😁😊

I discovered a lovely Okinawa soba shop in the Tomori area of Yaese Town. I visited “Okinawa Soba Senmon Shimabuku-ya” 🍜

The exterior is a warm, wood-based structure, and the sign says “Okinawa Soba Senmon Shimabuku-ya” in big letters. When you step inside the shop, it has the atmosphere of an old-fashioned shokudo, and a calm space where you can feel the warmth of wood spreads out before you.

The walls are covered all over with Okinawa soba menus, and with counter seats and table seats, it has an atmosphere that's easy to enter whether you're alone or with family.

Looking over the menu, the wide selection makes it hard to choose. From the classic soki soba to yushi-dofu (soft Okinawan tofu) soba and pig's feet (tebichi) soba, there are all sorts of variations. This time I chose soki soba without hesitation. My expectations rise, wondering what it will taste like 😄

What arrived was a soki soba topped with two impressive pieces of soki. The sight of it in a black ceramic bowl really whets the appetite. On top of the noodles are pork soki and green onions, and a side of shima rakkyo (island shallots) comes in a white dish.

Well then, let's start by tasting the soup. When I take a sip, the combined broth of bonito and pork bone spreads pleasantly in my mouth. What's especially striking is that the bonito broth really comes through, and the balance with the deep richness of the pork bone is exquisite. At first glance I thought it might be an ordinary shokudo-style taste, but I could feel an unexpectedly authentic harmony of broth 😋

Next, let's try the noodles. A thin, straight type, with a pleasant smooth texture 🍜

And the topping, the soki. For its size, it's finished in a way that has plenty of chewiness / bite. It has a sinewy texture and is finished with a light flavor 😄

And not to be forgotten is the side of shima rakkyo (island shallots). The color of the yellow shima rakkyo in the white dish brings a refreshing accent to the table.

Rating (9/15): What I felt overall is that “Okinawa Soba Senmon Shimabuku-ya” may look like an ordinary shokudo-style shop at first glance, but it's actually a shop serving quite authentic Okinawa soba. From the harmony of the bonito and pork bone combined broth, to the wonderful texture of the thin noodles, to the quality of the toppings, everything comes together at a high level. The side of shima rakkyo (island shallots) is lovely too, showing a thoughtful attention to detail. If you love Okinawa soba, I think it's a shop worth visiting at least once.
