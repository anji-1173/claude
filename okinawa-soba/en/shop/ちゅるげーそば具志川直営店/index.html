Churuge Soba Gushikawa Direct Store

ちゅるげーそば具志川直営店
Midori-machi, UrumaRating 9.5/15

In Midori-machi, Uruma, a sister shop of Ginowan Soba. Its specialty is the "Nuchi Masu Salt Bonito Soba," where the noodles and toppings come in the bowl without broth and you pour the stock from a teapot yourself. You can adjust the saltiness with Nuchi Masu, a specialty salt of Uruma, and it's hearty with three big soft-bone pork ribs.

I ate here in November 2023. This shop was a participant in the "13th Okinawa Soba Stamp Rally (Oct 1, 2023 to Feb 29, 2024)," and I went here as my 19th of 34 shops 😊

When you enter, there's a ticket machine near the entrance. This one also seems to be a cash-only ticket machine (;^ω^) I wish every Okinawa soba shop would go cashless soon.

This time I was after the "Nuchi Masu Salt Bonito Soba" featured in the stamp rally booklet 😊

"Huh?" I've seen this explanation of "rich" and "light" before lol. It's the same as at "Ginowan Soba," where I ate the other day.

Churuge Soba's explanation (light) says "chicken, pork, and bonito," so you can tell it's bonito-based. Ginowan Soba the other day says "three-stage preparation" yet only lists "chicken and pork," so the word "bonito" must have been left out (;^ω^) Because the taste definitely had a bonito base coming through lol

Churuge Soba's explanation of the noodles is also more detailed and thorough lol

However, for the "Nuchi Masu Salt Bonito Soba" I ordered this time, I wasn't asked which type of noodles or broth I wanted. Since the dish name includes "bonito," I assume the broth is the light stock.

While waiting for the "Nuchi Masu Salt Bonito Soba," I looked at the menu photos in the shop and saw a dish called "Gorogoro Soba," which I ate at Ginowan Soba the other day.

Between the explanations of the broth and noodle types, and, if you look closely, the back print on the staff's matching T-shirts, which reads "Okinawa soba specialty shops Ginowan Soba, Churuge Soba."

When I asked the staff after eating, it turned out it really is something like a sister shop of Ginowan Soba 😊 The dishes are almost the same as at Ginowan Soba too; the difference seems to be that Churuge Soba has a tofu shop in Uruma, so the "Yushi-dofu Soba" is what sets it apart from Ginowan Soba.

The "Nuchi Masu Salt Bonito Soba" arrived 😁 Surprisingly, the bowl had no broth in it. Apparently the stock is in a teapot.

Rather than the kettle at Yae Shokudo in Nago, this is the teapot version 😊

The staff asked me, "Is this your first time?" and explained how to eat it.

They said Uruma's specialty "Nuchi Masu" is mild, so it's fine to put it all in. However, they explained that I should first add about half and then adjust to my taste, so I ate it exactly that way.

As the staff explained, I added half the Nuchi Masu and then took a sip of the broth. As expected, it's a light, bonito-based broth. I tasted a little of the Nuchi Masu on its own, and it was just plain salty (;^ω^) But once it dissolves into the stock, it becomes a gentle, light flavor and is decently tasty. I thought it could be saltier without any problem, so I dropped in the remaining half of the Nuchi Masu.

The broth is a pretty high level. Next I decided to try the noodles hidden under the rolled omelet. These were wavy noodles. It seems the "Nuchi Masu Salt Bonito Soba" always comes with wavy noodles. These are also the same as the wavy noodles I ate at Ginowan Soba the other day.

The soft-bone pork ribs (nankotsu soki) are big, and there are three of them. It's nice that there's plenty of volume; the seasoning is gentle, and they're tender and decently tasty.

Rating (9.5/15): It's decently tasty. I thought the broth was a high level. This also seems to be a shop loved by locals; the parking lot in front of the shop fits a few cars, but it fills up quickly. Why not give this shop a try at least once too?
