Miyo-ya

みよ家
Kadena, Kadena TownRating 9/15

A homemade-noodle shop near the Kadena Rotary on National Route 58 and the town hall. A rare shop where you can taste the additive-free, light bonito-broth Zensei Soba, and "Tojin Soba" (with a black soy-sauce-based broth), the roots of Okinawa soba, revived after 110 years. Hugely popular with local customers.

― 2nd Visit (June 2024) ―

*This article was created with "ChatGPT 4o" and partly edited by me.

I went to eat here in June 2024 😄 Using the ¥500 discount voucher for Miyo-ya that I received at the Soba Jogu awards ceremony for the recent Okinawa soba stamp rally, I went to eat the "Tojin Soba" I'd been curious about for a while 😆

This "Tojin Soba" is said to be the roots of Okinawa soba, and it's a dish revived after 110 years.

Miyo-ya has an eye-catching storefront with colorful, very Okinawan decorations, and at the entrance hangs a sign reading "Homemade Noodles Okinawa Soba." As always, it was bustling with local customers.

You order easily inside the shop, receive a number tag, and wait at your seat. The wait wasn't that long, and the staff were kind and pleasant.

Inside the shop, there's a poster explaining the history and features of "Tojin Soba" in detail. "Tojin Soba" can be called the origin of Okinawa soba, and this soba, revived after roughly 110 years, is characterized by its special soy-sauce-based broth.

The "Tojin Soba" that was brought out is striking just from the look of its black broth. Taking a sip, I expected a strong soy sauce flavor, but it was a gentle taste with plenty of bonito flavor coming through. This is an irresistible point for lovers of Okinawa soba with bonito-based broth.

Next, the noodles. The homemade fresh noodles have a chewy texture and go perfectly with the broth. This chewiness is something special you can't get with store-bought noodles, and they're satisfying to eat.

The toppings include green onions and tofu-sized chunks of pork, letting you enjoy a flavor a little different from regular Okinawa soba. The flavor of this meat combined with the broth gave the whole bowl a very good balance.

"Tojin Soba" is a dish worthy of the word "revival," with the broth, noodles, and toppings each in fine harmony. It was also a nice plus that I could enjoy it for effectively ¥300 using the ¥500 discount voucher. It wasn't just decently tasty; it was a bowl where I could feel history and tradition.

Rating (9/15): The broth looks black and striking, like it would taste strong, but it has the usual Okinawa soba bonito broth coming through and is decently tasty. How about Miyo-ya, where you can taste "Tojin Soba," the roots of Okinawa soba?

― 1st Visit (October 2023) ―

I ate here in October 2023. This shop was a participant in the "13th Okinawa Soba Stamp Rally (Oct 1, 2023 to Feb 29, 2024)," and I went here as my 5th of 34 shops 😊

When I went inside, since it was lunchtime, it was bustling with local customers. There were quite a few elderly people too, so I thought it must be a shop popular in the community 😁

The "Zensei Soba" was featured in the stamp rally booklet, so I ordered the "Zensei Soba." The ticket machine is set up near the kitchen at the back of the shop, and you order with it.

When I asked, "Can I pay with my phone?" they said credit cards could be used, so I tried to pay by credit card, but whether it was a staff error or a machine malfunction, I don't know, the card payment wouldn't go through, and in the end I bought a ticket from the ticket machine with cash (;^ω^)

I continue to hope that cashless payment becomes possible at every Okinawa soba shop (;^ω^)

After a short wait, the Zensei Soba arrived. As always, a sip of the broth. It seems to be an additive-free broth, a light bonito stock. It's a gentle flavor with a subtle hint of bonito broth.

This broth is a light style reminiscent of the soba shops in the Shuri area of Naha that I ate at before. I thought it was tastier than the Shuri-area broth, but those who like rich, hearty broth might find it unsatisfying.

After the broth, I dig into the noodles 😊 You can choose between fresh noodles and boiled noodles, and I chose fresh. The medium-thick, slightly wavy noodles are chewy and delicious 🤗

Since it's "Zensei" (all-out), the toppings are rolled omelet, fish cake (kamaboko), braised pork belly (sanmai-niku), bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), and pig's feet (tebichi). The pig's feet are on the large side and wobbly.

Rating (9/15): The broth is on the light side, so those who like rich flavors may find it unsatisfying. It feels like a shop loved by local customers. My impression is that it takes a bit longer from ordering to serving than other shops. I also hope they get used to operating the credit card machine (;^ω^)
