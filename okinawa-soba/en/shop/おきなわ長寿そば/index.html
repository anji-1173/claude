Okinawa Choju Soba

おきなわ長寿そば
Sakimotobu, Motobu TownUniqueRating 9/15

A blue shipping-container shop on National Route 449 near the Shiokawa bus stop. Its specialty is "Choju single-noodle soba," where kishimen-like flat noodles form one continuous strand filling the whole bowl. Changing up the flavor with a chili-oil-crisp-style condiment is fun too.

I ate here in December 2024😄 I visited "Okinawa Choju Soba" in Sakimotobu, Motobu Town😊

This shop operates near the Shiokawa bus stop on National Route 449, and according to a newspaper article it's committed to making Okinawa soba with lots of local ingredients. The exterior is a vivid blue container type, with a presence you can spot right away even from a distance. The green parasols are a landmark, and the colorful, very Okinawan atmosphere is lovely🏝️

What I ordered from the menu this time was, of course, the famous "Choju single-noodle soba" (¥1,200)! The newspaper article said something like 4.5 meters, but looking at it in person, maybe it's not quite that long? But it's definitely not the length of a normal noodle~😅 This single noodle has a flat shape like kishimen, and it's amazing that it really is made as one strand!

When you lift it with your chopsticks, the way it just keeps going and going is hugely impactful. However, unlike regular Okinawa soba, honestly this flat shape makes it hard to slurp😆 The noodle texture is chewy.

The broth seems to be a combined broth with plenty of pork bone. The umami of the pork bone comes out well, and it's finished with richness🍲 The toppings are three kinds: pig's feet (tebichi), bone-in pork ribs (hon-soki), and braised pork belly (sanmai-niku). The tebichi is simmered until meltingly tender, full of collagen and delicious! The hon-soki has a sweetish seasoning and a bit of sinewy texture, but it's satisfying in its own way. The braised pork belly is also finished meltingly tender, with a nice balance of fat and lean. On the side there's fish cake, and interestingly, a condiment like chili oil crisp. Adding this partway through changes the taste of the broth so you can enjoy it two ways, which I think is a good idea🌶️

The location is Sakimotobu, Motobu Town, near the Shiokawa bus stop on National Route 449. Since it's a mobile food vendor, the hours and location may change, so it might be better to check in advance. There's parking for a few cars in front of the shop, but it's along the road, so be careful. The eating area is open-air, covered by a green tent, with colorful picnic tables lined up, so you can enjoy your meal in a resort mood. It looks especially pleasant on sunny days☀️

The menu is wide-ranging too, from the standard Okinawa soba to soki soba, yushi-dofu soba, and more, reasonably priced at ¥800 for medium and ¥950 for large. There are set menus too; the jushi set is ¥1,000 medium and ¥1,150 large. There's also an unusual "ASIA yakisoba" for ¥1,000, which piqued my curiosity🍝 The drink menu is extensive too, so you can enjoy very Okinawan drinks like Okinawa green juice smoothies and mango smoothies. Acerola soda and shikuwasa soda are Motobu Town recommendations as well, and it's nice to be able to enjoy local flavors🥤

Rating (9/15): The single noodle at "Okinawa Choju Soba" definitely makes for an impactful experience! The taste was plainly tasty too, and it was a warm shop where you can feel love for the local area. I think the unusual single-noodle style is worth experiencing at least once😊 It's a slightly different way of eating from regular Okinawa soba, but that was all part of a fun experience. When you're heading toward Motobu, please do stop by!🌺
